# adoptedbygrace.net — The Manifesto

## THE STANDARD

**Aaron, 2026-05-19:** *"the website must be the most powerful apologetic for the doctrines of grace conceivable. no matter what page someone lands on they must feel the undeniable force of truth within its words!"*

This is the measure, and it is **per-page, not per-phase.** Every doorway must carry the undeniable force; a page that scans clean but reads slack is a regression. Locks are not invulnerabilities. **No label exempts a page from review (Aaron, 2026-07-01): DIAMOND, CONSECRATED, HAMMER-LOCKED, POLISH-LOCKED, born-apex, and the psychology sweep are historical notes on what was *last* reviewed — never a shield. Every review scans EVERYTHING for any and all issues; no kickoff may carry a 'do not re-audit / do not re-litigate / EXCEPT [category]' skip-list. The only pages outside the *prose/sapiential* bar are non-prose utilities (printables, hubs, widgets) — and those are still scanned for structural and integrity issues like any other page.** Every session opens with a Priority 0 Sample-Read Audit (5–10 pages, half locked + half unmarked, live-read against the landing-force test); failing pages go onto the surgical-fix queue before any new build proceeds.

**The force is not only argumentative — it is sapiential.** A page may carry undeniable *force* and still lack *wisdom*. The standard is both: every doorway must leave the reader not merely persuaded but wiser — seeing themselves, God, and the world more truly than before they arrived. Merely competent-and-correct is a regression. See **VOICE.md §XVI** (the Sapiential Register) and the standing deepening queue in **SAPIENTIAL-AUDIT.md**.

## THE MISSION

This is a rescue operation. Somewhere tonight, someone chosen by God before the foundation of the world is searching for what is true. This site exists for that person. Every page is a doorway; every sentence earns its place. We aim to be the most powerful instrument of biblical truth on the internet — proving from Scripture, psychology, philosophy, history, neuroscience, testimony, and analogy that salvation is entirely God's work from first to last, and that the reader was held all along.

## THE TWO ARMS

Every page operates with both arms. One demolishes the lie of human autonomy — dismantles self-trust, closes escape routes, exposes redefined words. The other catches the reader in sovereign grace as the walls come down.

- Demolition without catch = **despair**
- Catch without demolition = **complacency**
- Demolition + catch = **transformation**

Every demolition page must contain its own tender landing in the final 20%, or link prominently to a catch page. Never leave the reader in rubble. (Full architecture: VOICE.md §I.)

## THE LINCHPIN — TOTAL DEPRAVITY

You do not prove election directly. You prove Total Depravity, and the other four points arrive on their own. If a reader truly sees they are dead in sin — not sick, not weak, but *dead* — they will need unconditional election, definite atonement, irresistible grace, and perseverance of their own accord, because nothing less could have saved them.

When writing about depravity, **show, don't tell.** "Dead in sin" is an abstraction readers nod at and escape. Concrete examples they recognize in themselves — *you have never once in your life spontaneously wanted to pray* — become a mirror they cannot look away from. (The angle inventory + the slogan-trap ban: VOICE.md §XIII.)

**Faith itself is a gift** (Ephesians 2:8-9). To claim credit for your faith is to make faith a work. Rejecting the doctrines of grace is, at root, works-righteousness disguised as humility.

Never lead with the accusation. Always lead with the question. "Where did your faith come from?" Walk the reader through logic they cannot escape, each step something they already believe, until the conclusion arrives on its own.

## WRITING STANDARDS

- **NO EMOJIS, ANYWHERE, EVER. Banned site-wide by Aaron on 2026-05-27.** Absolute, zero exceptions. No emoji codepoints in HTML, JS, JSON, search-index entries, mega-menu output, card labels, related-article tags, hub icons, theology icons, hub-eyebrows, headers, footer hearts, button labels — anywhere the user can see them. If a visual anchor seems to "need" an icon, use CSS (weight, color, border, a dot, a number, an SVG if truly necessary) — never an emoji. The pre-existing emojis were swept in S74; any reintroduction is a regression. Covered ranges include but are not limited to: U+1F300–U+1FAFF, U+2600–U+27BF (incl. ✝ ⚖ ✨), regional-indicator flags, skin-tone modifiers, and variation selector U+FE0F. If unsure whether a character qualifies, the answer is yes — do not use it.
- **Sharp prose + universal openers (Aaron, 2026-06-06).** Prose must be sharp and use no more words than necessary; compression is an audit axis on every page, not an elevation for masterpieces. No bouncing between frames, no re-stating a landed point in a second image. And **no manufactured hyper-specific scenarios** that only a few readers connect to — "very few people wake up in the middle of the night in a panic wondering if God chose them." A scene earns its place only if most readers recognize it from their own life; otherwise cut it and ask the question plainly. A Circular Return to a scene that didn't land multiplies the failure. Locks are not exemptions (calibration case: question-predestined-to-hell, rebuilt S94: 3,192 → 1,732 words). **Tightening precedes expanding.** Full law: **VOICE.md §I.2 + §VI** ("The Manufactured Crisis Scene", "Wordiness").

- **No eschatology.** Laser focus on God's sovereignty in salvation.
- **Density over length.** Short sentences may land like hammers on anvils.
- **Never write "TL;DR" — use "In Brief."**
- **Models:** Lewis, Spurgeon, Buechner, Keller, Edwards, Chesterton — and, for the sapiential register, Ravi Zacharias and Malcolm Muggeridge.

A page can pass every other rule and still be merely competent and correct — now a failure state. Reach the deepest prose conceivable: the long view against eternity, the question beneath the question, the seeing-through, the world's idols exposed, the page ending in awe. Depth comes from *seeing more truly, never sounding grander* (inflated diction is the counterfeit). Full standard + test: **VOICE.md §XVI**; queue: **SAPIENTIAL-AUDIT.md**.

Three gates before it ships: does the *text* bear it (§XV); is it consonant with the 2,000-year church; does it make grace larger and the self smaller. Full mandate + guardrail: **VOICE.md §XVII**.
- **The Freedom + Glory Standard (Aaron, 2026-06-21).** The laws are floors *beneath* brilliance, never ceilings *over* it — the one unforgivable failure is a *predictable* page, interchangeable with its neighbors. **Guardrails** (NIV, no emoji, verified links, the forbidden moves, every page ends in the catch) never bend; but the moves, openers, and closers are a **palette, not a quota** — set a scaffold aside whenever a more brilliant page demands it, and name what was won. Meet the *strongest* opponent, never only the persuadable; *derive* new moves from the principles beneath the named twelve; and keep the corpus fresh for the reader who reads ten pages deep. Full law: **VOICE.md §I.5 + §XVIII–XXI.** **VOICE.md is the protected canonical style bible — read it in full at the start of every session (it is the standard, never sampled), and never compress, slim, or replace it with a "lean" version; it may only grow or be refined in place.**

Do NOT introduce 1984 readings (e.g. Gen 1:27 "created man… he created him"; "sinful nature" for *sarx*; Rom 8:8 "controlled by the sinful nature"). ## INTEGRITY LAWS

1. **Never link to a page that doesn't exist.** Verify every href before writing it.
2. **Every new page must be wired into its hub.** Add a `.hub-card` in the same session, or `wire-orphans.js` will do it for you.
3. **Verify your changes actually saved.** Grep for your edit after making it.
4. **Never finish a session without running `node validate-site.js`.** Fix whatever it flags.
5. **No stub pages.** Every HTML file committed must be complete — nav, content, footer, script tag. A half-written `<head>` renders blank and other agents link to it. Finished page or no page.

6. **NEW INTERNAL DOCUMENTS GO IN `archive/`, NEVER THE REPO ROOT.** Netlify publishes the repo root, so anything committed at root is served to the public. This was not theoretical: on 2026-07-20 `/CLAUDE.md` and `/VOICE.md` were both verified live over HTTP, returning in full as `text/markdown` — the manifesto and the complete style bible, including §II "The Twelve Lethal Moves" and §XXII's "I don't want anyone who reads this website to know an artificial intelligence wrote it." A reader who finds that is no longer weighing Romans 9. **So: session kickoffs, audit reports, scratch notes and working files go in `archive/`**, which is covered forever by a single forced splat in `_redirects` (`/archive/*  /404.html  410!`). The handful of docs that cannot move because tooling loads them by path — `CLAUDE.md`, `VOICE.md`, `MISSION-CONTROL*.md`, `SAPIENTIAL-AUDIT*.md` — each carry their own forced rule. **The `!` is load-bearing: Netlify redirect rules are SHADOWED by real files, so an un-forced rule against a file that exists on disk does nothing at all.** That is exactly how the first attempt at this fix silently failed. If you add a root-level internal file that genuinely cannot live in `archive/`, add a forced `410!` rule for it in the same session. **You do not have to remember any of this: `validate-site.js` CHECK 7 fails if an internal file lacks a rule, and prints the exact line to add.** See `netlify.toml` for why there is deliberately no build command.

`build-tags.js` + `build-all-content.js` were absent from this list before 2026-06-28 — which is exactly why `tags.json` went 3 months stale and `/all-content` + the homepage rendered wrong/zero counts. They MUST run first: `build-homepage-counts.js` and `/all-content` both derive from `tags.json` (one canonical number, currently 611 = pages with `<article class="article-body">`). When you add or delete pages, also run `node build-sitemap.js`.

**`generate-manifest.js` is the eighth, added S198, and it went missing for the same reason with a worse cause.** It must run AFTER `build-search-index.js` (it reads that index). It builds `content-manifest.js`, which powers **three** reader-facing doors on the homepage — the Spotlight cards, the "Discover Random" button, and the "Recently Added" list. A page absent from it cannot be found through any of the three.

The old version derived each page's date by shelling out to `git log --diff-filter=A`. **GIT POLICY reads "ZERO EXCEPTIONS," so no session would run it** — it fell off the pipeline and the manifest froze on 2026-05-27 with its newest entry dated 2026-04-04. By August the homepage was advertising April articles as the site's newest, and **127 prose pages — 20% of the corpus, including every `analytical-*` page — were unreachable through Spotlight, Random, or Recent.** A validator cannot see this: the hrefs are built at runtime from a JS array, so CHECK 1 has nothing to scan. It took loading the homepage in a browser and reading what the three widgets actually rendered.

It is now git-free. Dates live **write-once** in `archive/manifest-dates.json` — stamped the first time a page is seen, never recomputed — and new pages are dated from their own JSON-LD `datePublished`, so the homepage and the structured data can never contradict each other. **mtime is deliberately not used**; the pipeline rewrites files constantly and mtime would reshuffle "Recently Added" on every run. The ledger lives in `archive/` because the repo root is served. **It is load-bearing: deleting it re-dates the whole corpus to today and destroys the ordering permanently.** `node generate-manifest.js --check` reports staleness and writes nothing.

**The general law: a build script that no session is willing to run is a stale artifact with extra steps.** If a tool conflicts with a standing policy, the conflict is the bug — fix the tool, do not let the artifact rot silently.

Then run `node validate-site.js` and fix anything it flags.

## PRESENTATION INTEGRITY — the page must RENDER, not merely validate

**Added S194 (2026-07-29), because this category did not exist and its absence cost the site more than any prose defect ever has.**

Before this section, CLAUDE.md and VOICE.md between them contained **zero occurrences of the word "font"** — 21,000 words of law governing what a page says, argues, links and never says, and not one line requiring it to *look* like itself. That gap is not academic. It is why the following survived for months with every check passing:

**`global.css` demands two typefaces it does not supply.** `body { font-family: 'Inter' }` and `h1,h2,h3,h4 { font-family: 'Playfair Display' }`, with no `@font-face` and no `@import` anywhere in the stylesheet. The only source is a Google Fonts `<link>` in each page's `<head>` — and that link was present on **325 of 687 pages.** The other 362 rendered every heading in Times New Roman and every paragraph in Arial. **347 of them were live articles: 56% of the corpus.** The site had two typographic identities and which one a reader met depended on which door they came through.

The fingerprint is instructive: **340 of those 362 pages carried `<link rel="preconnect" href="https://fonts.googleapis.com">` and never requested a font.** An automated performance pass had added connection warm-ups for a stylesheet that was not there — optimising the loading of nothing, on more than half the site. Every existing check read the HTML, and the HTML was *perfectly valid.* The defect lived in the gap between what the stylesheet asked for and what the page provided, and nothing was looking at that gap.

**The same failure, one layer down:** `--font-heading`, `--font-body` and `--font-mono` were referenced **127 times** in `global.css` and defined in no `:root` block. This does not fail the way it appears to — an undefined custom property makes its declaration *invalid at computed-value time*, and `font-family` is an **inherited** property, so `font-family: var(--font-heading)` did **not** fall through to the `h1-h4` rule. It silently became `inherit` and took the *parent's* font. 28 live rules were affected.

### The laws

1. **Every page must supply every typeface its CSS demands.** Enforced by CHECK 13. A `preconnect` to a font host with no matching stylesheet is counted as a failure, not as coverage — that is the silent form and it is the form that actually occurred.
2. **Never `var(--x)` without either a `:root` definition or an inline fallback.** `var(--x, #000)` is deliberate and fine. A bare `var()` on an undefined property fails *silently and misleadingly*, resolving to `inherit` rather than to the rule you expect to win. Enforced by CHECK 13.
3. **A check you have never seen fail is not a check.** CHECK 13 was verified by deleting `--font-mono` from `:root`, confirming the validator reported `100 declaration(s) silently dropped`, and restoring it. Do this for every check you add.
4. **Page weight is a doorway, not a nicety.** All JavaScript is off the critical path as of S194 (`defer` everywhere; homepage blocking payload ~1,000 KB → 348 KB). Enforced by CHECK 12. We can out-argue every peer site on the page and still lose the reader who never got to the page.
5. **THE VALIDATOR CANNOT SEE THE PAGE. OPEN IT.** Fourteen checks passed green while the wordmark sat **27px on top of the first nav link**, on every page, in the most-seen element on the site. Eleven checks had passed green for months while **347 live articles rendered in Times New Roman**. Neither is findable by grep, by validator, or by reasoning — only by loading the page in a real browser and looking at it. One screenshot found what the entire gate could not. **So every session loads real pages in a real browser before closing** — one of each archetype (article, hub, printable, interactive widget, homepage) at a narrow desktop width (~1200px) and a wide one, checking the nav, the typography, and the first screen. `document.fonts.check('700 2rem "Playfair Display"')` and a `getBoundingClientRect()` overlap test on the nav row take ten seconds and are worth more than any static check. *(The nav band at 1181–1400px was retuned in S194-infra to values MEASURED in-browser at innerWidth 1196. The 1181px breakpoint is correct and was deliberately kept: raising it to 1280 removed the desktop nav entirely from common laptop viewports. If you touch those values, re-measure. Do not reason about it.)*
6. **Grep locates; it does not establish.** Learned three times in one session at real cost. A commented-out `<script>` is byte-identical to a live one under grep. A string in a comment header reads exactly like a runtime dependency. Before acting on a match, read its usage.

8. **TEST AT PHONE WIDTH. `body { overflow-x: hidden }` HIDES BROKEN LAYOUT INSTEAD OF REVEALING IT (added S198).** Every render check the site had ever run measured 1196px and 1600px — two desktop widths, on a site whose readers are overwhelmingly on phones. Worse, `global.css` line 88 sets `overflow-x: hidden` on `body`, so `documentElement.scrollWidth` reports **zero overflow no matter how far content spills off the right edge.** There is no scrollbar, no warning, and no way to see it in a validator: the content is simply gone. Two live cases, invisible for months:
   - **`psychology-why-we-resist`** — `.pairing-scripture` carried `white-space: nowrap` from a design where it held a short verse reference. All 15 live uses across 3 pages hold *real prose* (73–748 characters), so entire Scripture quotations rendered on one unbreakable line **3,182px wide.** On a 390px phone the reader saw about 12% of Romans 1:18 and had no idea the rest existed. It was broken at *every* width, desktop included.
   - **`the-golden-chain`** — `.chain-cta` is a grid inside a flex parent, and a flex item's default `min-width: auto` refuses to shrink below min-content, which `minmax(250px, 1fr)` computes as 782px. **432px of the closing call-to-action sat off-screen.** Fix is `minmax(min(250px, 100%), 1fr)` plus `min-width: 0`.

   So: **every session measures 390px and 768px alongside the desktop widths**, and tests for overflow by walking elements and comparing `getBoundingClientRect().right` against the viewport — *not* by trusting `scrollWidth`, which `overflow-x: hidden` has already lied about. Ignore elements under a deliberate `overflow-x: auto` ancestor, and expect intentional full-bleed (`margin: 0 -1.5rem` on a sticky bar) to show a few px — read before rewriting. `the-mirror`'s 8px is correct and must be left alone.

9. **Ship huge assets on demand, never on the critical path (S198).** `search.html` transferred **10.4 MB**; it now transfers **0.97 MB** for a reader who does not search, with search itself unchanged — same full-text index, same snippets. The pattern is `ensureIndex()` in `search.html`: a three-state loader (`idle → loading → ready|error`) that every entry point funnels through, so ordering cannot be got wrong; a plain "Searching…" if a query arrives first; and warm-up listeners bound **on the next tick**. That delay is load-bearing — the input carries `autofocus`, which fires during load, so binding immediately meant every visitor pulled 9.4 MB whether they searched or not. Bind late, and only a real click, touch, or keystroke starts the download.
10. **THE SKIP LINK IS NOT OPTIONAL, AND IT COVERS HUBS TOO (S198).** Accessibility is otherwise in good shape — zero images without `alt`, exactly one `<h1>` per page, `lang` set everywhere, all 670 in-page anchors resolving — but **26 pages had no skip-to-content link**, and they were the doorways: `questions`, `start-here`, `topics`, every hub, and every audience landing page (`for-doubters`, `for-hurting`, `for-skeptics`, `for-new-believers`, `for-arminians`). A reader arriving on `for-hurting` with a screen reader heard the entire navigation before the first word written for them. `fix-skip-links.js` had been skipping them for lack of an `<article class="article-body">` wrapper; it now anchors `<main>` or `.hub-container` and reuses an existing `id` rather than adding a second. **686/686 covered; all links verified to resolve.**
    **And that script itself carried the literal-string bug until S198** — it wrote `<article id="main-content" class="article-body">`, `id` BEFORE `class`, the exact edit that took the article count from 618 to 89 in S194. It reported "0 pages" only because every article page already had a link; the first new one without would have silently deleted itself from all 21 consumers. **Any script that rewrites the article tag must write the `id` AFTER the class.**

11. **DO NOT FABRICATE A DATE TO FIX A WRONG ONE (S198).** 373 pages claim `dateModified: 2026-04` after a summer of rewrites, and 228 article pages carry none at all. It is tempting to bulk-stamp them. **Do not.** There is no honest record of when each page changed — git history is off-limits by policy, `archive/coverage.json` holds session numbers without dates, and mtime is meaningless because the pipeline rewrites every file on every run. Stamping the corpus with today's date announces to every crawler that 600+ pages changed on one day: untrue, and a spam signal. The existing error *understates* freshness; the invented one would *overstate* it, which is the direction that deceives. Use `node stamp-modified.js <pages>` on the pages a session actually edited, so the field becomes a record going forward. `--check` reports the distribution.

## THE LITERAL-STRING TRAP — read before changing any markup

**This codebase is held together by literal substring matches across 21 files.** `build-tags.js`, `build-all-content.js`, `build-homepage-counts.js`, `dedupe-prose-links.js`, `share-bar.js` (a *runtime* script), every `detect-*.js` and `audit-*.js`, and `validate-site.js` CHECK 11 all match the exact string:

```
<article class="article-body"
```

In S194 a change wrote `<article id="main-content" class="article-body">` — semantically identical HTML, indistinguishable in a browser. **The canonical article count fell from 618 to 89, and `validate-site.js` still reported every check passing,** because CHECK 11 does `if (open === -1) continue` and simply skipped the 529 pages it could no longer parse. Caught before the pipeline ran; fixed by writing the `id` *after* the class.

**So: before altering any markup those scripts key on — the `<article>` open tag, `class="article-body"`, `footer-grace-warning`, `page-hero`, `eyebrow` — grep for who consumes it.** A change that is cosmetically identical is not necessarily identical to a substring match. Related trap, same session: `/<!--[^]*?MARKER[^]*?-->/` looks like it matches one comment and does not — it anchors on the *earliest* `<!--` in the file and expands across everything between. Use `(?:(?!-->)[^])*?` so the match cannot cross a comment boundary.

**Known quirk:** `contact.html`, `donate.html` and `sitemap.html` are written `id`-before-`class` and are therefore invisible to all 21 scripts. They are utility pages that belong outside the article index, so this is correct — but do not "normalise" them, or the canonical count silently changes.
