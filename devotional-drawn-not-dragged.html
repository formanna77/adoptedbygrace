Drawn, Not Dragged

Grace did not override your will. Grace gave you a new one — one that wanted what it had refused.

In Brief

Your fear of irresistible grace is that you would be dragged to God — your will overridden, your personhood crushed, a marionette pulled along a string. That is not what happened. "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws them" (John 6:44). The Greek word is helkyō — a strong word for drawing. You were not hauled to Christ like a prisoner to court. You were drawn the way a healed body is drawn back to food — the hunger restored from within, the reaching finally your own. Your will did not lose. It was given what it had always lacked: the ability to want Him.

The Fear You Had

The fear had a specific shape. It went: if grace is irresistible, then I was not truly free. If I was not truly free, then my love for Christ is not real love. It is coerced love. It is the love a hostage gives a captor. And a coerced love is a lesser love. Therefore a God who operates by irresistible grace has produced, at best, a creation of puppets who only perform affection because the strings make them.

It is a beautiful-sounding fear. It has the rhetorical momentum of classical philosophy, enlightenment liberalism, and American individualism behind it. And it is completely wrong about how grace actually works.

The fear imagines grace as an external force overcoming an internal will — an army of God's bulldozers pressing against the fortress of your resistance until the wall falls and the bulldozers occupy the city. If that were the picture, the fear would be correct. That would not be love. That would be conquest.

But that is not the picture Scripture paints. Scripture does not describe grace as an external force that overwhelms the will. Scripture describes grace as an internal surgery that repairs the will. The will does not lose. The will is healed. A healed leg walks. A healed will chooses. And when a will is healed by grace, what it freely and joyfully chooses is the thing it would not have chosen when it was broken.

What John 6:44 Actually Says

"No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws them, and I will raise them up at the last day."

JOHN 6:44

The key word is draws. In Greek, helkyō — a strong word. It can describe hauling a net to shore or dragging a prisoner to court, so the gentleness of the pull is not the point John is pressing. The point is what the drawing does to the one drawn. Picture a man so sick that food has become his enemy. Set the finest meal in front of him and he turns his face to the wall — not out of defiance, but because the appetite is simply gone. You cannot argue him into hunger. You cannot command it. The want is dead, and a dead want cannot be scolded back to life.

Now heal him. Let the fever break and the strength seep back, and watch what becomes of the same plate he pushed away an hour ago. He reaches for it. No one forces his hand; no one drags the fork to his mouth. The reaching is entirely his own — and yet he did not manufacture the hunger that drives it. Health did. The appetite was given; the eating is his. He is not being fed against his will. He is feeding himself, because something underneath his will was repaired.

That is what the Father's drawing did to your soul. Before it, you had the capacity for God the way a starving body still owns a stomach — but no hunger, no orientation, nothing in you that reached. Worse: what appetite you had pointed everywhere but Him. To make you want Christ, God did not have to drag you to the table. He had to give you back your hunger. He set His Spirit into the dead want, and your soul did what a healed body does in the presence of bread. It reached. And the reaching was yours, even though the hunger was His gift.

Some hear that word draws as only a gentle wooing the soul may still wave away — and it is worth being honest that from the inside the drawing can feel exactly that resistible. But hear how Jesus finishes the sentence: "and I will raise them up at the last day." The drawing and the raising are bound together; everyone the Father draws, the Son raises. This is not a tug you finally out-stubborn. It is the kind of drawing that arrives.

This is why every converted believer, looking back, experiences the conversion both as something done to them and something they did. Both are true. God drew. You came. The drawing did not make you come against your will. The drawing was itself the repair of your will so that you could come. You experienced the coming as choice because by the time you came, the choice had been restored to you. The work that made the choice possible was done in a place deeper than choice — in the new creation of your inner life.

How He Draws

Think about how a parent draws an angry toddler out from under a table. The parent does not reach in and drag the child by the ankle. The parent kneels down. The parent speaks softly. The parent holds out a piece of the child's favorite food. The parent waits. Eventually the child, of their own accord, crawls out. The parent did not override the child's will. The parent created the conditions in which the child's will was won. But grace runs deeper than any parent's coaxing: God does not merely arrange persuasions and hope. The same Spirit who holds out the bread is the Spirit who restores the hunger for it — and so the drawing never fails.

This is a small picture of what grace does in the soul. God does not kick down the door of your heart with His boot. He does something more patient and more effective. He changes the atmosphere inside the house. He introduces scents you cannot help but smell — a little glimpse of the mercy of Christ in a sermon, a verse that stops you mid-reading, a kindness from a stranger that seems to have been arranged just for the day you were having. He tunes your ears until suddenly the passages you skipped over your whole life begin to ring. He tunes your eyes until the cross, which you had never really looked at, becomes the one thing you cannot stop looking at. And slowly, without ever ceasing to be you, you begin to want what you once found boring or offensive.

When the full drawing has happened, the soul comes out from under the table on its own legs. It is not being pulled. It is being won. And it could never have won itself: the same soul that now runs toward the light was, not long before, furious with it — wanting nothing of what it now wants more than anything. A change that deep is not self-made. The wanting was put there. The miracle is exactly that: I now want what I once refused, and I cannot explain the change except that He changed me.

The Love That Makes You Lovable

"I have loved you with an everlasting love; I have drawn you with unfailing kindness."

JEREMIAH 31:3

The drawing in Jeremiah is the same kind of drawing as in John 6, and look how it is described. With unfailing kindness. The Hebrew is chesed — covenant faithfulness, steadfast love, the love a groom pledges a bride. That is not the method of a tyrant. It is the method of a Lover, who moves first, moves patiently, moves with a wisdom so exquisite that by the time the beloved is in His arms she has long since stopped believing the journey there was against her will. Every small hunger for meaning you once wrote off as restlessness was His chesed, moving toward you with the patience of One who has forever to finish His work. You were not seized. You were wooed — and at last the wooing culminated in the gift of faith, the gift of saying yes with a whole heart. You said yes. You meant it. And the yes was the fruit of a courtship you never recognized as courtship, conducted by a Lover you could not yet see, who had been at work on your heart since before you had a heart.

Later the enemy will whisper that a love you did not originate cannot be real — that a freely chosen unbelief would be more honest than an imparted belief. It is one of the few angles that can still wound a believer rooted in sovereign grace. The answer is not to go searching your own chest for proof that your love is sincere enough to count; that is the old fear in a new coat, and it will always find the chest unconvincing. The answer is to turn the other direction entirely:

"We love because he first loved us."

1 JOHN 4:19

First. Your love is not the foundation; it is the echo. It rests on His and not the reverse — which means your assurance never hung on the strength of your own wanting, but on the One who wanted you before you could want anything at all. The drawing, the wooing, the waking, the coming — none of it was a hostage situation. It is the oldest love story in the universe, and you are finally, gratefully, in the arms of the Lover you were made for — the One who wrote the letter before time.

Drawn, not dragged. Kept, not coerced.
