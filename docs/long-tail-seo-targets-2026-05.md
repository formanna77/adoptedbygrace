## Why this matters

The technical SEO of this site is already excellent: 598 unique titles, a
unique meta description on every page, correct canonicals, rich JSON-LD
(Article, FAQ, Breadcrumb, Organization), an open robots.txt, and a clean
599-URL sitemap. The doorway is built. The reason traffic is thin is **not**
on-page — it is that the domain is young (migrated from `re-formed.netlify.app`),
has almost no inbound editorial links, and therefore cannot out-rank
decades-old authorities for head terms like *"total depravity"* or *"Calvinism."*

A young site does not win head terms. It wins the **long tail** — the oddly
specific questions the giants answer blandly or not at all, where your page is
simply the best answer on the internet. This site is unusually well-built for
that fight: 73 `question-` pages, 21 `objection-` pages, 8 `compare-` pages,
8 scripture-verse pages, and the Greek-word `apologetic-` studies are all
shaped exactly like long-tail queries.

## The two engines (do not confuse them)

- **The Google engine** — `question-`, `objection-`, `compare-`, verse, and
  Greek-word pages. These exist to rank for searches. This document is about
  them.
- **The human engine** — `essay-`, `devotional-`, `pastoral-`, `story-` pages,
  carried to readers via Substack (@ForeknownOne). These reach people who will
  never Google Reformed soteriology. Per `SUBSTACK-CROSSPOSTING.md`, question
  pages are correctly *excluded* from Substack. Keep it that way.

The engines do not overlap, and that is right. This list grows the Google engine.

### Original analysis (for reference)

Multiple pages targeting the *same* query split your ranking power, so Google
ranks none of them well. You have several clusters doing this. For each,
designate **one canonical page** for the head intent, then differentiate or
internally subordinate the others (link them to the canonical, retarget their
title to a distinct sub-query).

**"Can you lose your salvation / once saved always saved"** — 4 overlapping pages:

- `/question-secure` (Eternal Security)
- `/question-sealed` (Ephesians 4:30)
- `/question-book-of-life` (blot your name)
→ Recommend **`/question-perseverance` as canonical** for the head query;
retarget `secure` → "eternal security explained," `sealed` → the Ephesians 4:30
verse query, `book-of-life` → the Revelation/blotting verse query.

**"Is election fair / unfair"** — 3 overlapping:

## Title-tag alignment — the literary/searchable split

Your titles are beautiful, but the `<title>` tag is what Google reads hardest
and what a searcher clicks in results. A poetic title with no keyword cannot be
found. The fix is never to dumb down the page — keep the literary `<h1>` and
hero on the page; make the `<title>` tag search-shaped.

left untouched):

## Technical gaps (corrected after reading existing docs)

**Analytics — no change recommended.** Per `docs/ANALYTICS.md`, the site runs
server-side **Netlify Analytics** only, by deliberate, principled choice, and
the CSP (`connect-src 'self'`) enforces it. Do **not** add Google Analytics or
any client tracker. If the Netlify Analytics toggle (~$9/mo) is off, turning it
on is the cleanest way to see real pageviews, top pages, and referrers.

**Google Search Console — recommended, and CSP-safe.** GSC is not a client
tracker; it reads Google's own crawl data and verifies via DNS-TXT or an HTML
file (no page script, no CSP change). It is the one tool that will tell you
exactly which queries you appear for, your impressions and click-through, and
whether all 599 pages are actually indexed. This is the highest-value
measurement step and fully compatible with the privacy stance. Verify by
DNS-TXT or HTML file — *not* a meta tag — to keep pages clean.

**Sitemap `lastmod` honesty — fixed this session.** `build-sitemap.js`
previously stamped *every* page with today's date on every run, which trains
Google to ignore the freshness signal. It now preserves each page's real
last-changed date via a content-hash manifest (`.sitemap-hashes.json`); only
pages whose content actually changed get a new `lastmod`.

**Backlinks — the real off-page lever.** Substack reaches humans but its links
are largely nofollow, so they build little Google authority. The missing
ingredient is *editorial* links from other Reformed sites — being cited, quoted,
or linked by Monergism, blogs, pastors, and ministries. That is slow,
relationship-driven work, but it is what moves a young domain. No on-page change
substitutes for it.
