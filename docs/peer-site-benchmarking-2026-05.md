# Peer-Site Benchmarking — Where Adopted by Grace Stands in the Field

*Prepared 2026-05-21. Scope as requested: content/theological depth and persuasive/evangelistic force. Discoverability and design are touched only where they bear directly on whether the argument actually reaches a soul.*

---

## The Short of It

On the two things you asked me to measure — depth of content and persuasive force per page — Adopted by Grace is not a follower in this field. It is, page for page, very probably the most rigorous and most relentlessly persuasive site in the entire doctrines-of-grace space. I read our flagship apologetics against the actual published pages of the giants, and the giants are mostly writing 800-word explainers. We are writing 4,000-word demolitions that steel-man the Wesleyan, run the Greek of *ekzētōn* and *oude dynatai*, cross-check the claim against the ethnographic record, and then catch the reader in assurance before the page ends. Nobody else is doing that. Not at that length, not at that density, not with that architecture.

So this report is not a story of catching up. It is a story of a site that already out-argues the field and is missing four things the field has figured out: **a way in for the casual searcher, a reader-facing primary-source library, a graded learning path, and a second sense besides reading.** Each of those is a strength some peer has perfected, and each is importable without diluting a single thing that makes us strong.

The one genuine risk is the opposite of weakness. It is that our greatest strength — overwhelming density — is also a wall. The committed reader scales it and is changed forever. The 90-second searcher bounces off it. The field's best sites have all solved the on-ramp problem. We have solved the destination problem. We need both.

## Who I Studied, and Why They Matter

I grouped the field into five kinds of site, because they win in five different ways. Knowing *which* game each one is winning is the whole point — you cannot import a strength you have miscategorized.

### 1. The Ministries — they win on trust, voice, and warmth

**Desiring God** (John Piper). The most important site to study for *persuasive force*, because Piper solved a problem we have only half-solved. His entire project — "God is most glorified in us when we are most satisfied in Him" — makes the doctrines of grace *desirable* before they are *proven*. He leads with beauty and joy, not with the closing of an escape route. The doctrine arrives as the thing you most wanted, dressed as the thing you feared. The archive is vast (decades of sermons, articles, free books, the *Ask Pastor John* podcast), the voice is unmistakable, and the affectional register is the front door, not the back. Their strength is that they make sovereignty *attractive*.

**Ligonier** (R.C. Sproul's legacy). The most important site to study for *content architecture*. Ligonier built a teaching *institution*, not a content pile. `learn.ligonier.org` is a graded learning platform — beginner to advanced, with structured series ("What Is Reformed Theology?", "The Doctrines of Grace in John"), study guides, and *Tabletalk*, a daily devotional magazine with an enormous, loyal subscriber base. Their strength is the *on-ramp and the staircase*: a curious newcomer is handed a clear path from "what is this?" to systematic mastery, and a daily habit that keeps them returning.

**The Gospel Coalition.** A top-20 Christian website globally. Wins on *scale and distribution* — a coalition/network model with hundreds of authors, aggressive cultural engagement, daily content recaps, and global translation. Not a depth play and not a doctrines-of-grace specialist; it is broadly Reformed and broadly evangelical. Their strength is *reach and cultural relevance* — they show up in the conversation everywhere.

**Grace to You** (John MacArthur). Sermon-archive and broadcast model. Strength: a single authoritative expositor, decades of verse-by-verse preaching, radio/podcast distribution. Depth by accumulation rather than by per-page argument.

### 2. The Libraries — they win on primary sources

**Monergism.** The definitive *free* Reformed primary-source library: 1,400+ eBooks, Puritan and Reformation texts, Scripture-indexed, topically organized, with an FAQ and curated reading paths. Their strength is that they let a reader *stand inside the 2,000-year tradition* — read Owen, Edwards, Calvin in the original, for free. They are not trying to persuade; they are trying to *equip and preserve*.

**A Puritan's Mind.** Self-described largest Puritan/Reformed site since 1998. TULIP summaries braided with Puritan quotations, a huge free-eBook arm, and a publishing house (Puritan Publications) that re-issues old works under 175 pages for modern readers. Strength: *devotional depth through the voices of the dead saints*, plus an actual publishing pipeline.

**Banner of Truth / Reformed Books Online.** Puritan reprints and curated bibliography. Strength: *the canon, well-organized.*

### 3. The Answer Engines — they win on search and accessibility

**GotQuestions.** This is the most instructive contrast to us, so I read their "doctrines of grace" page in full. It is ~800 words. It defines the phrase, walks TULIP in one tight paragraph each with Scripture references, links every point to its own dedicated page, offers "For Further Study" books, "Related Articles," and ships in seven Bible translations with audio, video, print, dark mode, and translation into other languages. It is irenic, four-point, and *answers the exact question the searcher typed* in the time it takes to drink coffee. Their strength is **discoverability plus accessibility**: they rank #1 for nearly every theological question a human types into Google, and they reward the click with a clear, short, complete answer.

**CARM** (Matt Slick). Apologetics framing — ready-made arguments, Early-Church-Father quote collections supporting the doctrines of grace, debate-oriented, and notably staffed by *both* Calvinist and non-Calvinist writers (which buys credibility). Strength: *equipping the reader to argue and to meet objections head-on.*

**Reasonable Theology.** Clean beginner explainers. Strength: *the gentle first explanation.*

### 4. The Confessional / Ecclesial — they win on rootedness

**Founders Ministries** (Tom Ascol). Reformed Baptist, confessionally anchored (Abstract of Principles, 1689), with a seminary and pastor-training institute. Strength: *the doctrines of grace tied to the confessions and worked out in the life of the local church* — worship, witness, ecclesial formation. Authority by confession and institution.

### 5. The Opposition — the case we exist to answer

**Soteriology 101** (Leighton Flowers, "Provisionism"). The leading anti-Calvinist voice online, podcast-driven, with a "former 5-point Calvinist" credibility hook and a respectful-debate posture. He has real reach inside Southern Baptist circles. This matters enormously for us: a great many searchers hear the *anti*-sovereignty case first, in audio, on a long drive, from a likeable former insider — and *then* go searching. We already engage him directly (`response-leighton-flowers.html`), which is exactly right. But note *how* he wins: emotional objections (fairness, God's love for all, free will, "evangelism becomes pointless"), delivered conversationally in a medium we don't use. His strength is **multimedia reach plus emotional framing of the objections.** Our objection-* and demolition-* pages out-argue him in text. The question is whether the searcher ever reaches the text.

## Head to Head: Adopted by Grace vs. the Field

### Where we lead — and it is not close

**Per-page argumentative density.** I compared our `apologetic-the-prayer-you-never-spontaneously-prayed` against GotQuestions, Ligonier articles, and Desiring God's five-points piece. Ours runs many times their length and carries a fundamentally heavier payload: a behavioral diagnostic the reader runs on their own memory, three named "pretender categories" answered in turn, a steel-manned Wesleyan counter dismantled on three independent grounds (ethnographic, doctrinal, Christological), Greek exegesis of the load-bearing verbs, and a tender landing in assurance. No peer writes at this level of sustained pressure. GotQuestions is a dictionary; we are a courtroom.

**The two-arms architecture.** Almost every peer is *one-armed*. Desiring God catches (warmth) but rarely demolishes. CARM demolishes (argument) but rarely catches. We do both on the same page — close every escape route, then catch the reader in sovereign grace as the walls come down. This is genuinely rare and it is our deepest competitive moat. Demolition without catch is despair; catch without demolition is complacency; we are built to deliver transformation.

**Multi-disciplinary proof.** No peer proves total depravity from neuroscience, psychology, philosophy, economics, addiction science, language acquisition, *and* Scripture. Our `secular-*`, `psychology-*`, and `philosophy-*` wings (some 68 pages) attack the same doctrine from angles the giants never touch. This is a category we essentially own.

**The crown-jewel argument.** "Where did your faith come from?" — faith as gift, credit-for-faith as works-righteousness — threaded site-wide and led with as a *question*, not an accusation. I have not seen any peer build their whole site around this single inescapable hinge. It is our sharpest evangelistic instrument and it is unique to us.

**The hyperlinked web.** 594 pages with an 8–12-link minimum and cross-category linking turns the site into a living web rather than a filing cabinet. Monergism has more *documents*; nobody has a more *interconnected* original corpus.

### Where we are at parity

**Breadth of topical coverage.** Our systematic-theology wing (34 pages), history wing (24), and comparative wing match or exceed most peers' doctrinal coverage. We are not behind on *what* is covered.

**Persona on-ramps.** `start-here.html` already routes the curious / skeptical / wounded reader to different doorways — conceptually close to Ligonier's learner-pathing, though persona-based rather than difficulty-graded.

**Layered summaries.** "In Brief" appears on 235 of 594 pages — a real strength that bridges toward the GotQuestions accessibility model. It is just not yet universal.

### Where we are behind — the four importable strengths

1. **The on-ramp for the 90-second searcher (the GotQuestions strength).** Our density is a wall for the casual visitor. Peers win the searcher with a short, scannable, complete answer up top. We have "In Brief" on 40% of pages; the other 60% open straight into deep water. The searcher who wanted a two-minute answer bounces — and bounced searchers are souls we never reached.

2. **A reader-facing primary-source library (the Monergism / A Puritan's Mind strength).** This is the most glaring and the most fixable gap. We *own* 114 curated Reformed PDFs in `/Reformed Sources/` — and **zero** reader-facing pages link to them. They are used to write the site but readers cannot access them. Monergism's entire authority comes from letting people read Owen and Edwards directly. We are sitting on a comparable asset and hiding it. Surfacing it would let our reader "stand in the 2,000-year tradition" exactly as the mission statement promises.

3. **A graded learning path (the Ligonier strength).** Our `start-here-phase1–5` pages hint at a curriculum but it is not presented as a coherent, trackable staircase from "I just heard about election" to "I can defend definite atonement from Hebrews." Ligonier's clear beginner→advanced track and daily-devotional habit loop are why newcomers stay and mature. We have the raw material for the best such curriculum in existence; we have not yet assembled it as a path.

4. **A second sense besides reading (the Desiring God / Ligonier / Soteriology 101 strength).** The site is text-only — 16 of 594 pages carry any audio or media. This is partly deliberate, and the written word is rightly our spine. But every major peer reaches people through ears as well as eyes, and our most effective *opponent* reaches them almost entirely through audio. The searcher driving home from work cannot read us. A simple audio reading of flagship pages (the crown-jewel argument, the best devotionals) would open a channel the opposition currently owns uncontested.

## Recommendations — Prioritized

These are ordered by *return on effort for the mission* — souls reached per unit of work — not by difficulty.

**Priority 1 — Surface the Reformed Sources library.** Build a reader-facing library page that exposes the 114 PDFs, organized the way `REFORMED-SOURCES-INDEX.md` already organizes them (author, era, topic). Link to relevant primary sources from the foot of every doctrinal article ("Read the tradition: Owen on definite atonement"). This converts a hidden internal asset into Monergism-class authority overnight, and it is almost pure upside — the material already exists. *Highest return, lowest cost.*

**Priority 2 — Make every page survivable in 90 seconds.** Drive "In Brief" to 100% coverage (it is at 40%), and ensure each long page opens with a scannable promise of what the reader will gain. Keep the depth — just put a door in front of the wall. This directly attacks our only real weakness: the bounce of the casual searcher. Think of it as a layered page — *the GotQuestions answer on top, the courtroom beneath.*

**Priority 3 — Assemble the graded curriculum.** Turn the `start-here-phase` pages into an explicit, visible staircase: a named track ("The Path Into Grace," beginner→advanced) with a sense of progress, that gathers existing pages into a deliberate sequence. This is the Ligonier on-ramp built from content we already have.

**Priority 4 — Lead with beauty more often (the Piper move).** We catch beautifully, but we usually catch *last*, after the demolition. On a subset of front-door pages, experiment with leading with the desirability of sovereign grace — joy, rest, being-held — and letting the argument follow the longing. Our `joy-hub` and devotional wing prove we can do this; the question is sequencing it to the *front* on the pages a newcomer hits first.

**Priority 5 — Open the audio channel.** Even a plain spoken-word reading of the 20–30 flagship pages would contest the medium our opposition dominates. Start with the crown-jewel argument and the strongest devotionals. (Note: this revisits the prior "no TTS/video" decision — worth a deliberate reconsideration given that audio is precisely where Soteriology 101 reaches the undecided.)

**Priority 6 — Own the search results we deserve.** Our `question-*` wing (74 pages) is already built around the exact phrasings people type — this is the right instinct and a real asset. Make sure those pages are structured to win the featured snippet (clear question as H1, tight answer first, depth below). We out-argue GotQuestions on every shared topic; we should also out-rank them, or at least show up beside them.

## The One Thing No Peer Has

Every site I studied is winning a *partial* game. Desiring God makes it beautiful but rarely closes the escape routes. GotQuestions answers the question but never moves the heart. Monergism hands you the library but never argues. Ligonier teaches the system but in a measured institutional register. CARM arms you for the fight but leaves you cold. Soteriology 101 reaches the ears but gets the doctrine wrong.

Adopted by Grace is the only site attempting *all of it at once on every page* — beauty and argument, demolition and catch, Scripture and neuroscience, the searcher's question and the saint's assurance. That integration is the moat. The four strengths above are not corrections to a failing site; they are the missing on-ramps to a destination that is already, page for page, the strongest in the field. Build the doors. The house is already the best one on the street.
