   <h2>Who the Gardener Is</h2>

   <p>There is one more thing about Him, and the story has been circling it from the first page.</p>

   <p>On the first Easter morning a woman stood outside a tomb in a garden, crying, and a man asked her why. She did not recognize Him. John reports the detail without comment, and it is the tenderest mistake in the Bible: "Thinking he was the gardener, she said, 'Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have put him.'"</p>

   <p>She was wrong about who He was. She was not wrong about what He does.</p>

   <p>He had already said how the work gets done, and He said it in farming: "unless a kernel of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains only a single seed. But if it dies, it produces many seeds." Nothing in that is gentle. A buried seed is not resting; it is coming apart in the dark, softening and splitting until it is no longer the thing it was, and no seed has ever agreed to it. That is the only way a harvest has ever started.</p>

   <p>So the Gardener <a href="/systematic-atonement">went down into the soil Himself</a>. And centuries before He did, Isaiah wrote what was waiting on the other side of it: "Yet it was the Lord's will to crush him and cause him to suffer, and though the Lord makes his life an offering for sin, he will see his offspring and prolong his days, and the will of the Lord will prosper in his hand."</p>

   <p><em>He will see his offspring.</em> Read that as a parent. The crushing was not a catastrophe God salvaged afterward; it was a sowing, and the Man being sown was told in advance what would come up out of Him. Every child who has ever turned around and walked back up a driveway was inside that sentence — promised to the Son, not owed to the parents. Which is exactly why it holds. It was never addressed to you, so it cannot fail with you.</p>

   <p>You cannot make anything come up, and you were never given that job. Keep the soil turned. Pray the prayer you are tired of praying. And on the nights you feel nothing at all — no faith you can find, no hope you can work up, no sense that heaven is even in the room — the seed in the ground is not waiting on your belief in it.</p>

   <p>The Man who was buried in that garden knows exactly how long a thing can lie under the earth and still come up.</p>

<p>You are what He came up carrying.</p>
REPLACEMENT:
<p>Jesus said that about Himself, days before He was buried. He is not only the one who plants. He is the seed that went into the ground, and the whole field is what came up with Him.</p>

   <p>Which unmakes the flowers' story at the root. They were not lying; they were reasoning from the only evidence in reach, and a flower is built facing the wrong way to get this one right. Every part of it turns toward light. The one direction it cannot look is down — and if it could, there would be nothing there to see. The seed was spent making the flower. That is why self-made feels so obvious from the inside: the proof of where you came from was used up producing you.</p>

   <p>And He would not leave it in the past tense. "I am the vine; you are the branches. If you remain in me and I in you, you will bear much fruit; apart from me you can do nothing." Not <em>you could do nothing once</em> — nothing now, this afternoon, in the middle of your most competent hour. A cut flower holds its color for days. Color proves nothing about a root.</p>

   <p>You are not what grew itself. You are what He came up carrying.</p>

<p>It is finished.</p>
REPLACEMENT:
<p>Obtaining. Not offering. Not making available. <em>Obtaining.</em> Eternal redemption — secured, purchased, finished. For you. Before you drew breath. By a Savior who is <a href="/devotional-never-gives-up">not in the habit of letting go</a>.</p>

   <p>That one word is what all the careful language above it exists to protect. The two natures, the one person, the vocabulary the church spent centuries getting exact — none of it was built by men who loved precision for its own sake. It was built around the image Jesus chose in John 12 to describe His own death: a kernel of wheat goes into the ground, and a harvest comes up. Any farmer can tell you what that relation means. The size of the yield was settled long before the field — settled when the seed was chosen.</p>

   <p>Which puts every question about His person on a very short leash. Ask how much came out of that grave and you are only asking what went into it. A merely remarkable man is a seed that yields a remarkable man, and the arithmetic stops there. A God who merely appeared to die was never planted, and nothing unplanted has ever produced anything. Both mistakes sound generous toward Jesus. Both quietly reduce the harvest, and you would find out by how much on the one day it is too late to ask.</p>

   <p>So the two natures were never a riddle for admiring; they are the precise shape a Savior has to be for a burial to yield a world. Which is why the doctrine goes on working on the day you cannot. When there is no faith you can locate, no repentance you can summon, nothing inside you that feels like belief, the harvest is not being computed from you.</p>

   <p>Everything you are was in that seed.</p>
