<p>Stop on the last clause. <em>Even as I am fully known.</em> The archetypal knowledge you have been demanding — total, immediate, nothing sealed — already exists. It is God's knowledge of <em>you</em>. It has been running since before the foundation of the world, and it has never once been neutral: it is the knowing of a Father who <a href="/devotional-chosen-before-you-were-broken">chose what He knew</a>, every ruin included. You will never comprehend Him. You have always been comprehended by Him. The fully-known one is not God. It is you.</p>

   <p>And the &ldquo;face to face&rdquo; that is coming is not the moment the roster unseals and you finally audit the decree. It is communion, not capture. Eternity will be the unending reading of an inexhaustible God — every age a deeper page, no bottom, no boredom, no final chapter, world without end. Heaven is not where you at last comprehend Him. Heaven is where you never have to stop beholding Him.</p>

   <p>You were not asked to know everything. You were asked to know Him — and before you knew anything at all, He knew you, whole, and stooped, and stayed. The whisper in your hands is true. Follow it home.</p>

=== PAGE: apologetic-ekloge-the-greek-of-election

ANCHOR:
<p>This article is for the reader whose heart is stirred even now to ask. The stirring is itself a sign. Stones are not stirred by the Greek of Romans 9; the dead do not weep at <em>eklogē</em>. <a href="/question-depravity">The fallen mind in its pure state finds these doctrines repellent</a>; the response of the natural man to Romans 9 is to slam the book and leave. If the book is still open in your hands, if some part of you is bending toward the verbs and not away from them, then <a href="/question-where-did-your-faith-come-from">the bending is itself a gift</a>. The bending is the Spirit's first work. The asking is on its way.</p>

   <p>Ask. Plainly. <em>Lord, if You have chosen me, give me eyes to see it. If You have not yet drawn me, draw me now.</em> The Greek of Romans 9:16 says the mercy does not depend on the asking; it depends on the One who shows mercy. But the mercy that does the showing usually shows itself, in this life, by giving the asker the words to ask in the first place. The Greek does not work the other way. The Greek works in only one direction, and the direction is from the Caller toward the called, from the One who shows mercy toward the one whose only contribution is to be there when the mercy arrives.</p>

   <p>The Caller is calling. The Greek does not bend. The mercy is His to give, and He has been giving it longer than you have been alive.</p>
END_ANCHOR
REPLACEMENT:
<p>There is one more entry in the lexicon, and the argument above walked straight past it. The word <em>eklogē</em> is a choosing, and a choosing is never a bare act; it comes down on somebody. Follow the root out of Romans 9 and watch where it lands first. On the mountain, in the voice out of the cloud, the Father names His Son <em>ho eklelegmenos</em> — the one who has been chosen (Luke 9:35). Same stem. Perfect participle: the choosing already done, and standing done. Before the word was ever laid on a sinner, it was worn by Christ.</p>

   <p>Which means it has been pointing somewhere this page has not yet followed it. Read Ephesians 1:11 again and stop on how it opens: "In him we were also chosen, having been predestined according to the plan of him who works out everything in conformity with the purpose of his will." <em>En hō</em> — in whom. Ephesians 1:3-14 is one sentence in the Greek, and it will not stop repeating the phrase: in him, in him, in him. Paul never lets the chosen stand loose in a category. He puts them inside somebody. And 2 Timothy 1:9 says where the grace was kept before there was a world to keep it in: "This grace was given us in Christ Jesus before the beginning of time." Election was never a name on a list. It was a people lodged in a Person, so that whatever holds Him holds them.</p>

   <p>So you can stop taking your own temperature. Whether these verbs move you or leave you cold settles nothing; <a href="/question-depravity">the natural response to Romans 9 is to close the book and walk out</a>, and God has overruled that response in every believer who has ever lived. Faith, when it comes, <a href="/question-where-did-your-faith-come-from">arrives as a gift and never as an achievement</a>.</p>

   <p>Which leaves one thing to do, and it takes no equipment. Ask. Plainly, in your own words: <em>Lord, if You have chosen me, give me eyes to see it. If You have not yet drawn me, draw me now.</em> That prayer has never once reached a reluctant God. The choosing was finished before the world had a shape, and it was not sealed in a room you cannot enter — it was set inside a Son, and the Son came out into the open where a beggar could reach Him. You have been searching your own interior for proof that the choosing included you. It was never kept there. He is where God keeps His chosen.</p>
END_REPLACEMENT
NOTE: The page was already carrying the fix — eklogē is a choosing, and Scripture never leaves the chosen floating: it wears the word on Christ first (Luke 9:35) and locates the chosen "in him" (Ephesians 1:11, 2 Timothy 1:9) — so the close now walks the lexical method one turn further into the Person instead of landing three times on "the Greek," and the old feeling-certificate ("the stirring is itself a sign… the bending is the Spirit's first work") is replaced by its opposite, which is what redirects the reader from his own interior to the Savior.
