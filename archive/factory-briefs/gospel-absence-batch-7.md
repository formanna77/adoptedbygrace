```html
<cite>PHILIPPIANS 1:6</cite>
   </blockquote>

   <p><em>He who began.</em> Not you. Not your feelings. <em>He.</em> And what He began, He will finish — not if you feel enough, but because He has never stopped being God.</p>

   <h2>A Prayer for the Numb</h2>

   <p><em>God, I feel nothing. My prayers feel like they stop at the ceiling. My Bible feels like paper and ink. My faith feels like a memory of something I used to have.</em></p>

   <p><em>But I am here. Something in me won't let go — or something won't let go of me. I am choosing to believe it is You, even though I cannot feel You choosing me.</em></p>

   <p><em>So hold me in the dark. Not because I feel Your hands, but because You promised they would be there. Not because my faith is strong, but because Yours is. I am taking You at Your word tonight. It is all I have left — and if Your word is true, the silence is not empty. It is full of a God I cannot see, holding a child who cannot feel Him, and <a href="/devotional-never-gives-up">refusing to let go</a>. Amen.</em></p>

   <blockquote>
   <p>"Where can I go from your Spirit? Where can I flee from your presence? If I go up to the heavens, you are there; if I make my bed in the depths, you are there."</p>
   <cite>PSALM 139:7-8</cite>
   </blockquote>

   <p>The silence is not absence. It is the quiet of a Father still in the room — the friend who sits beside you in the hospital after the news and says nothing, because there is nothing left to say and everything left to give.</p>

   <p>He is here. You cannot feel Him. That is all right. Feelings are weather; He is climate. The light on the window comes and goes. The house has never moved.</p>

   <p>Your salvation does not rest on how you feel about Him tonight. It rests on how He feels about you — and He has felt the same way about you since before He made the stars. He looked down the long hall of all the nights you would ever have, saw this one, and said <em>mine</em>. Then He went and died to make the <em>mine</em> stick.</p>

   <p>In the morning the numbness may still be there. Or it may lift the way weather lifts, without explanation. Either way He will still be holding you — because the holding was never conditional on your feeling it.</p>
```

```html
<p>The assumption underneath: <em>you</em> were the one steering, and you fumbled it.</p>

   <p>But ask this. If your obedience had been the determining factor, where would it have come from? Where does any obedience come from? Either God works it in you (Philippians 2:13 — "for it is God who works in you to will and to act") or it comes from what Paul calls the mind governed by the flesh, which "does not submit to God's law, nor can it do so" (Romans 8:7). Either God gives the obedience or there is none. If God gives the obedience, the seasons He did not give it were not failures of <em>your</em> sovereignty. They were the unfolding of <em>His</em>.</p>

   <p>This is not a license to wander. It is release from self-blame for years never yours to control. The locusts did not come because you left the gate open. The Sovereign over locusts had a vineyard to plant on the other side of the famine. You did not derail His plan. You walked the road He laid down — even the parts you would not have chosen, even the parts you cannot forgive yourself for, even the parts where you felt alone.</p>

   <p>You were not drifting. You were being carried. The current you thought was carrying you away from God was the current He set in motion to bring you home through the long way around. Years lost to wandering are not always a detour from your calling. Sometimes they are the long way the calling had to travel home. None of it happens any other way, because the God who chose you before the foundation also chose the road. And the road went through the wreckage on purpose.</p>

   <p>The question is no longer "how do I forgive myself?" It is simply this: did the God who wastes nothing waste those years? He did not. And that answer does not wait on your believing it. The road did not require your approval while you were walking it, and the harvest does not require your permission now. You are not asked to make the years count by deciding they counted. You are asked to look at what was being built during the seasons you were certain nothing was.</p>

   <h2>Back to the Wreckage</h2>

   <p>Look again at those years. The marriage. The addiction. The exile. The illness. The decade you would delete. Not one fact about any of it has changed. What has changed is who was in the room.</p>

   <p>You are not behind. You were never off course. The locusts had orders too.</p>

   <div class="reflection-box">
   <h3>A Moment with God</h3>
   <p>Father, I have been grieving years I thought were lost. I have been carrying the weight of time I believed was wasted — seasons of failure, of running, of breaking.</p>
   <p>Teach me to see what you see: not wreckage but ingredients. Not waste but preparation. Not chaos but sovereign design. Not a story with missing chapters but a story where every chapter was necessary — even the ones I would have skipped.</p>
   <p>You chose me before any of it. You are using all of it. And you will finish what you started.</p>
   <p>I trust you with the years I cannot account for. They are yours. They always were.</p>
   <p>Amen.</p>
</div>
```

```html
<h2>The Storm May Come Back</h2>

   <p>It may. Not the same storm, probably, but another one. A doubt. A sin. A terror in the night. You should know that the quiet you are in this morning is not a promise of uninterrupted weather. It is a promise of a Master who speaks to weather. When the next storm comes, you will not have to calm it yourself. You will not have to hold the boat together. You will call on the One who has already stilled one storm in your life, and He will still this one too. He is, after all, in the boat. He has been sleeping in the stern this whole time. Wake Him. He will rise. He will speak. The wind will stop.</p>

   <p>For now, though, the wind has stopped. Sit in it. Drink your coffee. Let the morning be what it is. You have come out of a storm you did not know how to calm, into a calm you did not know how to produce. That is grace. Let it.</p>
```
