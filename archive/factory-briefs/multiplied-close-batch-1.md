<p>To believe that your salvation revolves around your choice is to believe that you must sustain it. You must keep it alive. You must manage it. You must guard it. Your eternal destiny rests on your will, your faithfulness, your decision to stay committed. The pressure is immense. You are at the center, and that center must hold.</p>
   
   <p>But what if the center is not you?</p>
   
   <p>What if God is the center, and your faith orbits around His sovereign choice? What if you were chosen <a href="/question-chosen">before the creation of the world</a>? What if the Spirit has already drawn you, already illuminated you, already granted you faith as a gift? What if your salvation doesn't rest on your shoulders but on His?</p>
   
   <p><strong>Suddenly, you are free.</strong></p>

   <p><strong>You no longer have to sustain yourself.</strong> The gravitational force that holds you is not your will. It is God's grace. And it has been working since before time began.</p>
   
   <p class="footnote">This is not permission to sin. This is not an excuse for spiritual apathy. <a href="/devotionals">The truth of grace—rightly understood—produces the deepest repentance and the most radical obedience</a> precisely because you are free from the terror of self-salvation.</p>
   
   <div class="pull-quote">
  Being removed from the center isn't demotion. It's the only position from which you can finally rest.
   <div class="pull-quote-attribution">— The Peace of Dependence</div>
</div>
   
   <p>This is the experience of everyone who has lived both sides of this revolution. They build intellectual towers to defend God—towers that require them to be the center, the one who can reason his way to faith, who can choose and decide and control. And God shatters them. He brings a person to the place where there is nothing left but surrender—where the only choice is to accept that there was no choice, that they had been chosen long before their choosing could even be possible.</p>
   
   <p>And in that surrender, he found not diminishment but glory. Not powerlessness but peace. Not insignificance but the unspeakable privilege of being held by infinite love.</p>
   
   <p>That is the Copernican Revolution of the soul. Not dethronement. Deliverance.</p>

   <div class="section-body">
   <p>Tomorrow morning — if God grants you tomorrow — the sun will rise in the east and set in the west. It will look exactly the way it has always looked. The sensation of standing on firm ground will be indistinguishable from the sensation you had before you knew the Earth moved. Nothing will <em>feel</em> different.</p>

Because you will know that what feels like standing still is actually hurtling through space at sixty-seven thousand miles per hour, held in orbit by a gravitational force so vast and so gentle that you cannot feel it working. You will know that the sunrise is not the sun moving toward you but <em>you</em> being turned toward the sun — slowly, inevitably, by a force that was set in motion long before you existed.</p>

   <p>That is <a href="/question-how-grace-feels">what grace feels like</a> from the inside. It feels like your own decision. It feels like you reached for God. It feels like you are standing still and the gospel is moving toward you. But the truth — the Copernican truth, the truth that rewrites the model without changing the experience — is that you were being turned. Drawn. Orbited into the light by a gravity you did not create and cannot resist. And the vertigo you feel right now, reading this, is not the ground giving way. It is the ground <em>finally becoming real</em> beneath you — because for the first time, you are standing on something that does not depend on you to hold it up.</p>

   <p>The sun rises. You orbit. And the God at the center is not diminished by your smallness. He is glorified by it. Because what He holds, He holds forever.</p>
