## apologetic-his-own-purpose-and-grace   (1914w)

**OPENER**

> In Brief: Most debates about election argue over why God chose. Paul, in one sentence, settles it by telling us when : "He has saved us and called us to a holy life — not because of anything we have done but because of his own purpose and grace. This grace was given us in Christ Jesus before the beginning of time" (2 Timothy 1:9). Two locks slam shut at once. The first is the merit-lock: not because of anything we have done — the cause is "his own purpose" (Greek prothesis , God's settled, sovereign plan). The second is the time-lock: the grace was given — the Greek is an aorist, a finished act — "before the beginning of time" ( pro chronōn aiōniōn , before the ages began). And that second lock is the one that finishes the argument, because it dates the gift to a moment before you, before your faith, before any deed good or bad, before time itself. A grace handed over before you existed cannot have been earned by anything you did. Even your

> You were chosen in Him, so that there has never been a moment, including the timeless moment before all moments, in which the elect were not already the Father's gift to the Son. The Date on the Gift Is Older Than Your Sin So if you are weary tonight of wondering whether you have believed hard enough, repented sincerely enough, held on tightly enough — if your assurance rises and falls with the temperature of your own heart — hear what the date does to your fear. The grace that saved you was not given on the morning you finally got your act together. It was given before the beginning of time . ## apologetic-i-know-whom-i-have-believed   (2151w)

**OPENER**

> In Brief: "...because I know whom I have believed, and am convinced that he is able to guard what I have entrusted to him until that day" (2 Timothy 1:12). Paul writes this from a Roman prison, abandoned by friends, awaiting execution — and he says he is not ashamed. Why? Read the grammar of his confidence. He does not say "I know what I have believed" — he says "I know whom. " His security is a Person, not the firmness of his own conviction. And the reason that Person can be trusted is named: "he is able to guard what I have entrusted to him." The word "entrusted" is parathēkē , a banker's term — a deposit handed over for safekeeping. When you deposit something in a vault, its safety no longer depends on your strength but on the vault's. Paul has deposited his very soul with God, and rests not in his grip on the deposit but in God's power to keep it "until that day" — the day of Christ. So the question for your own

**CLOSE**

> power. The thief who could break into that vault has not been born and never will be. The night the Guard falls asleep at His post has never come and never will. You deposited your soul with God, and the safety of a deposit is the strength of the keeper, not the strength of the one who handed it over. This is why a dying man in chains could write "no cause for shame" with the calm of someone watching the sun come up. He was not staring at his own faith and hoping it would hold; he was looking at his Lord and knowing it would. And the same is offered to you, in whatever your version of the cold cell is — the failure you cannot forgive yourself for, the doubt that visits in the dark, the fear that this time you have finally drifted too far. The soul you entrusted to Christ is being guarded by Christ, and He has never once lost what was placed in His hands. "I shall lose none of all those he has given me," He said — and He does not misplace souls. He keeps what He is given, all the way to that day. The deposit is safe — not because your grip is strong, but because the Keeper is.

## apologetic-no-one-righteous   (2126w)

**OPENER**

> In Brief: Almost every testimony runs on the same hidden premise — I was a seeker. I was looking, asking, searching, and at the end of my search I found God. Romans 3 detonates the premise in the flattest sentence in Scripture: "There is no one righteous, not even one; there is no one who understands; there is no one who seeks God" (Romans 3:10-11). Not few seek. Not most seek poorly. No one. The Greek piles negation on negation — ouk estin... ouk estin... oude heis , "there is not... there is not... not even one" — and the participle ekzētōn means the diligent, intense seeking-out of God Himself. Paul's verdict on the natural human search for God is that it does not exist. What you called your seeking was one of two things: a search for God's gifts — peace, meaning, a better self — which is not seeking Him ; or it was the sound of God's own footsteps already behind you. "The Son of Man came to seek and to save the lost" (Luke 19:10) —

**CLOSE**

> not the search party scanning the wilderness. You were the missing person who did not know they were missing, and the Shepherd left the ninety-nine and came over the ridge for you. Feel the weight lift, because it is enormous. If finding God had depended on the quality of your seeking, you could never rest — there would always be the question of whether you sought hard enough, sincerely enough, long enough, and the dread that a better seeker might find a God you missed. But the verse takes the search out of your hands and puts it in His, and His search does not fail. He did not wait at the end of a road hoping you'd walk it. He came down the road after you. The same God who is strong enough to declare that no one seeks Him is strong enough to seek and to find every single one He came for — and if you have been found, it is because He was the one looking. Your whole testimony, retold truthfully, has a different hero. It was never the story of a clever sheep who found the Shepherd. It was always the story of a Shepherd who would not lose His sheep. You were not the search party. You were the one He came to find.

## apologetic-the-everlasting-covenant   (2084w)

**OPENER**

> In Brief: The doubter's deepest fear about perseverance is not will God keep His promises? but will I keep mine? Jeremiah 32:40 answers by making the believer's own faithfulness God's promise: "I will make an everlasting covenant with them: I will never stop doing good to them, and I will inspire them to fear me, so that they will never turn away from me." Notice the two clauses. God guarantees His side — I will never stop doing good — and then He guarantees yours: I will inspire them to fear me, so that they will never turn away. The Hebrew under inspire is natan , "I will put my fear in their hearts" — He implants the very reverence that keeps them, and the "so that" names its designed result: they will not turn away . The thing you were terrified depended on you is the thing God has covenanted to produce in you. Perseverance is real — and it is real because preservation is prior. You hold on because you are held. Ask a trembling Christian what frightens

**CLOSE**

> in the guarantee; it is part of the guarantee. Look at the clause again: I will inspire them to fear me, so that they will never turn away from me.
