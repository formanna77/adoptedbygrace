   <div class="objection-header">&ldquo;This makes God responsible for evil.&rdquo;</div>
   <div class="answer-body">
   <p>This objection assumes that sovereignty and <a href="/objection-responsibility">moral responsibility</a> work the same way for the Creator as they do for creatures. They don&rsquo;t. An author who writes a villain into a novel is not guilty of the villain&rsquo;s crimes. A playwright who scripts a betrayal has not committed betrayal. The Creator-creature distinction means God can ordain events for purposes that are holy even when the events themselves, from the creature&rsquo;s perspective, involve evil.</p>
   <p>Scripture says this explicitly: &ldquo;You intended to harm me, but <strong>God intended it for good</strong>&rdquo; (Genesis 50:20). Same event. Two intentions. One evil (the brothers&rsquo;), one good (God&rsquo;s). Sovereignty doesn&rsquo;t make God the <a href="/objection-author-of-sin">author of sin</a>. It makes Him the author of a story in which sin serves purposes the sinners never intended.</p>
   <p>For a deeper exploration of this: How Can a Sovereign God Not Be Responsible for Evil?</p>

   <div class="section" id="punchline">
   <h2>The Punchline the Skeptic Didn&rsquo;t See Coming</h2>

   <p>So next time someone leans back and asks, &ldquo;Can God make a rock too heavy for Him to lift?&rdquo; &mdash; smile. Because they&rsquo;ve just handed you the most elegant argument for God&rsquo;s absolute sovereignty ever constructed.</p>

   <p>Their own question establishes the principle: God cannot do what is logically self-contradictory. And a created universe that escapes its Creator&rsquo;s control is precisely that &mdash; a self-contradiction. To ask God to make something outside His sovereignty is to ask Him to be God and not-God in the same breath. And <a href="/philosophy-problem-of-merit">the problem of merit</a> dissolves entirely when we understand that merit presupposes human capacity independent of God, which sovereignty nullifies. A nothing pretending to be a something.</p>

   <p>The &ldquo;rock&rdquo; question was meant to tear down God&rsquo;s power. Instead, it proves that His sovereignty over all things &mdash; including you, including your salvation, including the faith that stirs in your chest right now &mdash; is not just a theological claim. It is a <em>logical certainty</em>.</p>

   <div class="verdict-box">
   <div class="verdict-quote">God cannot make a rock too heavy to lift. And He cannot make a soul too far gone to save.</div>
   <p style="color: var(--text-secondary); line-height: 1.8; margin-top: 1rem;">Both are impossibilities for the same reason: they ask omnipotence to negate itself. But here&rsquo;s the difference &mdash; the rock question is a parlor trick. The salvation question is <a href="/devotional-never-gives-up" class="cross-ref gold">the best news you&rsquo;ve ever heard</a>.</p>
</div>

And the God who holds the universe holds you &mdash; not because you asked, not because you chose, but because He cannot stop being what He is. And what He is, is Sovereign. Even over you. Especially over you.</p>

<p>This is the perseverance of the saints — which is really the perseverance of <em>God</em>. Your salvation is not as secure as your grip on God. It is as secure as God's grip on you. And His grip does not slip.</p>

   <h2>Why This Matters More Than You Think</h2>

   <p>There are only two options for how salvation works. Either God is the decisive cause, or you are. There is no middle ground — a deciding sliver, however small, still makes you the hero of the story, not God.</p>

   <p>The doctrines of grace say: God is 100% the cause. <a href="/question-faithgift">Faith itself is His gift</a> (Ephesians 2:8-9). <a href="/question-where-did-your-faith-come-from">You did not produce it.</a> You received it from the same God who chose you, bought you, called you, and will keep you. From first to last, salvation is the Lord's.</p>

   <p>This is not cold theology. This is the warmest comfort in the universe. If your salvation depends on your decision, then every moment of doubt, every season of weakness, every dark night of the soul puts your eternity at risk. But if your salvation depends on God's decision — on the sovereign, unchanging, irrevocable choice of the One who <a href="/devotional-never-gives-up">never gives up on His own</a> — then nothing in all creation can separate you from His love.</p>

   <p>This is not a modern invention. Augustine preached it in the fifth century — we are not elected because we first believed, but elected so that we might believe. <a href="/theologian-luther">Martin Luther</a> staked the Reformation on it. <a href="/theologian-edwards">Jonathan Edwards</a> watched God use it to wake New England. Spurgeon thundered it to thousands every Sunday. These truths are not a theological opinion held by a faction. They are the heartbeat of every genuine awakening the church has ever known — because when the church discovers that God saves, the church catches fire.</p>

   <p>That is what the doctrines of grace teach. Not five abstract theological propositions. One breathtaking reality: <a href="/devotional-rescued-without-a-say">you were rescued</a> by a God who would not take no for an answer — because He loved you before you existed, and He will love you after the stars burn out.</p>

   <p class="landing-line"><strong>His grip does not slip.</strong></p>
