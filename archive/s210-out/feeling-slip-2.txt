REPLACEMENT:
<p>Read the most uncomfortable sentence on this page slowly: <strong>you would rather be damned on your own terms than saved on His.</strong> That preference is the whole shape of the flesh. It is why reprobation makes you angry and election feels like an offense — and it is what Paul means by "<a href="/question-depravity">dead in sin</a>": not unconscious, but running a courtroom against God every day of your life. Yet here is the terrifying mercy. God does not wait for the prosecution to rest. The man who wrote that phrase about the dead was carrying warrants up the Damascus road with the next arrest already planned when the case was taken out of his hands — nothing softening first, nothing in him leaning the other way, nothing a court could have called evidence. Put the gavel down. You were never going to sustain the case.</p>
END_REPLACEMENT
NOTE: The slip was "If you can see the prosecutor at all, you are not him... Something else in you is watching him now, and is tired of his files. That something was given to you" — self-awareness offered as evidence of regeneration, and a flat contradiction of this same page's later refusal of "a certificate issued on the strength of a mood"; repaired with move 1, relocating the ground outside the reader (Saul still carrying warrants when the case was taken out of his hands, with nothing in him to work with) inside the page's own courtroom image, and leaving the later section to own the explicit refusal so nothing is said twice.

   <p>But if — quietly, almost too quietly to hear — you say <em>His. It was His all along</em> — then the room will exhale. The centuries will smile. And you will look down at your own hands and see, for the first time, that they are not white-knuckled around anything. They are open. They have been open for a long time. REPLACEMENT:
   <p>And if you say <em>His. It was His all along</em> — however flatly, however unconvinced — the room will exhale, and the centuries will lean back. But notice what your answer did not do. It did not open your hands. Look down at them: they are not white-knuckled around anything, and they have not been for a long time. Someone untangled them while you were not looking, and finished before you had a word to say about it. The man who sits in that box with nothing to say at all — no confession he trusts, no feeling to report — is looking at the same hands.</p>
END_REPLACEMENT
NOTE: The slip was the tender conditional "But if — quietly, almost too quietly to hear — you say His... then the room will exhale... And you will look down at your own hands and see, for the first time, that they are open" — the reader's confession, graded on its quality, made the trigger for both the welcome and the seeing, on a page whose entire argument is that the faith was never his; repaired by keeping the courtroom and the untangled hands but relocating the ground (the hands were open before he had a word to say) and covering the silent reader explicitly in the same breath.

=== PAGE: systematic-reprobation
VERDICT: REPAIRED
ANCHOR:
   <p>This is the right question. And the answer is one of the most beautiful doctrines in all of Reformed theology.</p>

   <p><strong>If you fear you might be reprobate, you almost certainly are not.</strong></p>

   <p>The reprobate do not fear reprobation. The reprobate are at peace with their distance from God. The reprobate find church boring, prayer pointless, Scripture dull, the gospel unimpressive. The reprobate scroll past articles like this one without a second thought. The reprobate do not weep over the possibility that God might have passed them over. The reprobate are far too comfortable in their own autonomy to be troubled by the question of whether God chose them.</p>
END_ANCHOR
REPLACEMENT:
   <p>This is the right question, and it usually gets the same answer: the reprobate do not fear reprobation, so your fear clears you. <strong>That certificate will not be issued here.</strong> Not because the observation is false — the settled reprobate scrolls past an article like this one without registering that he did, and has never once wept over the possibility that God passed him by. It will not be issued because a certificate has two sides. Hand a frightened man his own dread as proof, and in the same motion you have handed a diagnosis to the numb, the medicated, the exhausted and the constitutionally unmoved. No one sitting in a pew was ever given the standing to enter that judgment on himself.</p>
END_REPLACEMENT
NOTE: DEPARTS FROM THE PRIOR, but on a different sentence than the prior ruled on — the sanctioned "The wanting is not your proof; it is His fingerprint" paragraph and the "Fear is a ruinous instrument" paragraph are both left untouched; the unrepaired slip sits upstream of them, in the bolded "If you fear you might be reprobate, you almost certainly are not" plus its six-sentence checklist ("The reprobate find church boring, prayer pointless, Scripture dull, the gospel unimpressive"), which is a verdict issued on a mood, is reversed by the page's own next paragraph ("the answer is not to go examine the fear... Take no reading from it"), and convicts every numb or depressed reader before that qualifier arrives; repaired by naming the move and refusing it in the same breath while keeping the true observation, so the following sanctioned paragraph now deepens the refusal instead of contradicting the page. Also drops an announced-wonder line ("one of the most beautiful doctrines in all of Reformed theology") and nets three paragraphs down to one.
