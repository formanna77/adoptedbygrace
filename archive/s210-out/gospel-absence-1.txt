If you are His, you are not His because a hypothetical version of you in a hypothetical world freely chose Him under circumstances He happened to find convenient. You are His because He said your name before He said <em>let there be light</em>, purchased you before you drew breath, and will keep you by the same sovereign word that made the stars stand still in their sockets. The word that called the worlds into being is the word that calls you home.

REPLACEMENT:
One more look at Ephesians 1:11 — this time from its first words, not its middle. <strong><em>"In him we were also chosen."</em></strong> Not in a world. In <em>him</em>. Election has an address, and the address is not an arrangement of circumstances. It is a Person.
</p>

   <p>
  Which is also why the plan was never going to be the efficient one. Middle knowledge is, at bottom, an optimization — locate the feasible world where the most come freely at the least cost. The cross is the opposite of an optimization. If salvation were a matter of finding the right arrangement, there was no reason under heaven for it; a God who works by arranging circumstances could have arranged His way around it. He did not. The plan He spoke ran straight through His own Son, because the result was never the point. We were.
</p>

   <p>
  So if you are His, you are not His because a hypothetical version of you in a hypothetical world freely chose Him under circumstances He happened to find convenient. You are His because before the world had a shape God chose you in His Son — and a Son is not a circumstance that can be rearranged. Every rival account of your salvation makes you the product of an arrangement. This one makes you someone Christ died to have.
</p>

   <p>
  The plan was never a world. It was a Son.

REPLACEMENT:
The objections are answered. The question remaining is whether you will let the answers in — or whether you will reload a sixth objection to keep the wall standing.</p>

   <p>One more thing about the weapon, before you decide. The round most often fired at everything on this page is John 3:16 — the verse nearly everyone can recite, raised as proof that the decisive move belongs to us. Read its main verb. <em>"For God so loved the world that he gave his one and only Son, that whoever believes in him shall not perish but have eternal life."</em> He <strong>gave</strong>. The sentence people reach for to defend their own contribution turns out to be a sentence about a gift — and the gift is not a doctrine, or a system, or an arrangement that sounds fair. It is a Son.</p>

   <p>So the wall was never built against a position. Positions have no name and no face. Romans 8:7 named your posture before you arrived: <em>"The mind governed by the flesh is hostile to God."</em> That is a strange thing to say about a disagreement and an exact thing to say about a fortress. And Christ did not lay siege to it. He walked into range of men who were hostile to Him — unarmed, knowing precisely what they would do — and let them do it, and He did it for them, while their objections were still loaded.</p>

   <p><a href="/start-here-phase5">Phase 5</a> is waiting for the person who sets down the weapon. Understand what that surrender actually is. Not the losing of an argument. The meeting of the One the argument was always about, who has already done the costly part, and who is not standing outside your wall waiting for it to fall before He will have you.</p>

   <p>Set it down. He never came armed.
END_REPLACEMENT
NOTE: The figure is the weapon that was never a weapon — John 3:16, the round the objector fires from memory, is itself a sentence about a Son being handed over, and the Christ outside the reader's fortress came unarmed into range of men Romans 8:7 calls hostile; the page had already built the fortress, the loaded objection, and "you are the rescued," so the Savior only had to be placed inside its own siege.
