```html
<p>Thomas was in the garden. Of course he was. The sunflower was still there — taller than him now, leaning toward the house.</p>

   <p>He looked at the sunflower. The one he didn't plant. The one that arrived as a gift from a Gardener who works in soil that human hands have not tilled, on schedules that human calendars cannot contain. The October light was low and golden, and the sunflower had turned its face toward the house — as if it had been waiting, too, for this exact moment to point its gaze where the warmth was.</p>

   <p>His hands were shaking. Dirt under the nails, same as the day the phone call came. He typed back with soil-black fingers: <em>Always, son. I'm here.</em></p>

   <h2>The Truth Behind the Story</h2>

   <p>Scripture teaches that regeneration — the new birth — is a work of God alone. "The wind blows wherever it pleases. You hear its sound, but you cannot tell where it comes from or where it is going. So it is with everyone born of the Spirit" (John 3:8). No parent, no pastor, no friend can produce <a href="/question-faithgift">saving faith</a> in another human being. The faithful parent plants and waters. The <a href="/systematic-soteriology">sovereign God</a> — in His own time, by His own means — gives the growth.</p>

   <p>And <a href="/question-perseverance">what He begins, He finishes</a>. <a href="/devotional-never-gives-up">He does not give up</a> on what He planted. The hope that survives a prodigal is not hope in the prodigal's return. It is hope in the Gardener — the one who works soil no human hand has tilled, and brings things up in His own time. If He chose your child before the foundation of the world, the story is not over.</p>

   <h2>Who the Gardener Is</h2>

   <p>There is one more thing about Him, and the story has been circling it from the first page.</p>

   <p>On the first Easter morning a woman stood outside a tomb in a garden, crying, and a man asked her why. She did not recognize Him. John reports the detail without comment, and it is the tenderest mistake in the Bible: "Thinking he was the gardener, she said, 'Sir, if you have carried him away, tell me where you have put him.'"</p>

   <p>She was wrong about who He was. She was not wrong about what He does.</p>

   <p>Jesus Christ rose in a garden, and the first person on earth to see Him alive took Him for the man who tends the ground. He had already told them why: "unless a kernel of wheat falls to the ground and dies, it remains only a single seed. But if it dies, it produces many seeds." The Gardener went into the soil Himself. That is how anything grows here at all.</p>

   <p>So this is who you are waiting on. Not a distant sovereignty that may or may not get around to your child. A <a href="/systematic-atonement">Man who has been under the ground</a> and knows exactly how long a thing can lie buried and still come up.</p>

   <p>Keep the soil turned. Keep praying the prayer you are tired of praying.</p>

   <p>He came out of a grave Himself.</p>

<h2>Why This Changes Everything</h2>

   <p>If Christ's atonement is definite — if He died specifically for you, personally, by name, before you existed — then your salvation was never a coin flip. It was never hanging in the balance, waiting for you to tip the scales with your choice. The scales were settled at Calvary. The blood was sufficient. The verdict was pronounced. And every link in the <a href="/systematic-ordo-salutis">chain that followed</a> — your calling, your <a href="/systematic-regeneration">regeneration</a>, the <a href="/question-faithgift">faith you thought was yours</a>, your justification, your ultimate glorification — flows from that one definite, purposeful, accomplished act on the cross.</p>

   <p>He is not a teacher offering suggestions. He is God in flesh, accomplishing what no human being could accomplish, for people who could never have accomplished it themselves.</p>

   <blockquote>
   <p>"He entered the Most Holy Place once for all by his own blood, thus obtaining eternal redemption."</p>
   <cite>HEBREWS 9:12</cite>
   </blockquote>

   <p>Obtaining. Not offering. Not making available. <em>Obtaining.</em> Eternal redemption — secured, purchased, finished. For you. Before you drew breath. By a Savior who is <a href="/devotional-never-gives-up">not in the habit of letting go</a>.</p>

   <p>The receipt was signed before you arrived.</p>

   <p>Stop and feel what that means. Before your mother knew she was pregnant. Before the doctor handed her your weight. Before the first time you cried in the dark wondering if anyone would ever come — your name was already on a piece of parchment in heaven, and the ink was the blood of God. You were not an applicant. You were not a candidate. You were not a possibility He hoped would work out. You were a name Christ said out loud at Calvary, in a voice the universe heard, the moment His head fell forward and the curtain split in two. He did not die for a hypothesis. He died for <em>you</em>. By name. With intent. On purpose. Forever.</p>

   <p><em>Lord Jesus, in whom all things hold together — hold me. I have nothing to add to what You finished. I stop trying to add. I stop trying to earn. I stop trying to ratify. I receive what You sealed for me before I was born. Let the word You spoke from the cross be the word I fall asleep inside of tonight. Tetelestai. Amen.</em></p>

   <p>It is finished.</p>
```
