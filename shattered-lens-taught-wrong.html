The Shattered Lens

When Everything You Were Taught Was Wrong

For the one who opened their Bible and found a God they had never been introduced to.

By Foreknown · January 1, 2026

In Brief: A verse will not sit still, and the ground moves: what if much of what you were taught about your part in salvation was wrong? What is coming out from under the house is sand, and sand was never going to hold it, however solid it felt underfoot. And underneath the vertigo is the question nobody says out loud — what if He did not choose me? — which Christ answers not from your interior but from a door He has never once shut. He chose before you could, gave the heart that believed, and holds what He took hold of. The floor that fell out is not the last floor.

It started with a verse you had read a hundred times. This time the words would not sit still. They rearranged themselves while you watched.

"You did not choose me, but I chose you."

Your stomach dropped. You read it again. You tried to make it say what you had always been told it said. It would not cooperate.

One verse led to another. You opened Romans 9 to settle your mind and it unsettled you further. Ephesians 1 kept saying "before the creation of the world." John 6:44 said something that stopped you cold: "No one can come to me unless the Father who sent me draws them." Not invites. Not offers. Draws. The sentence has a subject, and the subject is not you.

The Bible you have read your whole life is saying something you have never once heard from a pulpit. And you are alone with it.

Notice what your body is doing right now. Maybe something in your chest tightened two paragraphs ago and has not let go; maybe nothing happened at all. Either way, nothing your chest is doing settles anything. But watch the part of you that wants to close this tab and go back to the version of God that did not unsettle you. That part is not protecting your faith. It is protecting your throne.

The Ground Beneath You Is Moving

This is not a theological disagreement. This is a worldview collapse.

You grew up believing something simple and beautiful: God loves everyone equally. Jesus died for every person. You just need to choose Him. That was the bedrock. Your church reinforced it, your own heart embraced it, and it felt true because it felt good.

Then you started reading the Bible for yourself, and the Bible started contradicting your teachers. Election, predestination, calling, drawing, choosing — the verbs keep pointing away from human choice and toward divine action. You try to contextualize Romans 9, to spiritualize it, to make it say something less devastating. Paul will not let you: "Does not the potter have the right to make out of the same lump of clay some pottery for special purposes and some for common use?"

Vertigo. Betrayal. The sensation of falling through truth you thought was solid.

Someone will hand you the answer that is supposed to settle it, and it deserves stating at full strength, because serious people have held it for centuries. Romans 8:29: "For those God foreknew he also predestined to be conformed to the image of his Son." Foreknew comes first. So God looks down the corridor of time, sees who will believe, and predestines those. Election is real on this reading. It is simply informed.

Then press on the verb. The Greek is proegnō, and its object in that sentence is not a fact but a person. God does not foreknow that they would believe; He foreknows them — the knowing of Jeremiah 1:5, where God knows a man before forming him in the womb. Covenant intimacy, not advance information. And even granting the other reading, it moves the question one room over: if God foresaw your faith, something in you produced the faith He foresaw, and you are holding the one asset you had to bring and had to already have.

And you cannot unsee it. You catch yourself arguing under your breath, defending the old theology even as the new one takes root.

You are not crazy.

You Are Not Losing Your Faith — You Are Finding It

What feels like deconstruction is actually construction.

Jesus described two foundations in Matthew 7: sand and rock. The house on sand looks great in fair weather. It accommodates your preferences. It lets you believe what makes you feel good. But when the storm comes, it collapses. The house on rock is built on truth that cuts against your comfort — on a God who is bigger than your understanding of how love should work.

So the question is not what your vertigo proves. Your vertigo proves nothing; a man can be dizzy on solid rock. The question is what the ground was made of. The old foundation was sand. Sand cannot hold a house, and no amount of standing very still on it will turn it to stone.

Notice the grammar of what is happening to you. Every verb in it is a cannot. You cannot stop reading these passages. You cannot dismiss them. You cannot go back to the comfortable version. Nobody has ever willed himself into being unable to look away. Jesus described a Guide who works exactly like that:

"But when he, the Spirit of truth, comes, he will guide you into all the truth."

JOHN 16:13

He guides. He makes the truth irresistible. A false foundation coming out is not the opposite of a house going up. It is the first hour of it.

Ask yourself this — gently, because the answer will change everything: Who opened your eyes to these verses? Not who told you about them. Who made you unable to unsee them? You did not wake up one morning and decide to find sovereignty in the Bible. You opened the book and sovereignty found you. Did you choose that? Or did it choose you?

Be careful here, because there is a cheap version of this argument and it will fail you the first time you feel nothing. The cheap version says: your wrestling proves you are alive. But your own heart is the one witness Scripture will not let you call — deceitful above all things (Jeremiah 17:9) — and there will be weeks when the wrestling stops and the numbness comes and you will need something steadier than your own pulse. The real question is not whether you are stirred. It is whose voice made a dead man turn his head at all. You did not generate that. It was done to you.

And you are afraid you are losing your faith. What you are losing is the part of it that had you holding the house up. The house was never yours to hold.

Why No One Told You

Your pastors and teachers were not lying. Most of them ran into the same resistance you are feeling now and stopped where you are being asked not to. A salvation your choice controls is comforting, because it is yours. Being chosen before the creation of the world is terrifying, because it is not. So they read Romans 9 and contextualized it, and preached around the chapter rather than through it.

The word of God is alive and active, sharper than any double-edged sword. It cannot be contained forever. It gets out. It finds people. It found you.

The God You Are Meeting Is Better

You were taught that God had done everything He could and the last move was yours. Stated carefully — and it is often stated carefully — that is prevenient grace: a love that runs ahead of you, unbinds a will you could never have unbound yourself, and then genuinely leaves the answer with you. That is not a mean doctrine. It is guarding something worth guarding, which is that love compelled is not love.

Follow it one step. Grace goes out to two men in equal measure. One believes. Whatever finally separated them came from inside one of them — call it humility, openness, a lower resistance — and he produced it out of the same nature the other man had. The one asset he had to bring, he had to already have. Which is the question the whole of Romans 9 exists to answer, and the answer it gives is not he was more willing.

"For he chose us in him before the creation of the world to be holy and blameless in his sight."

EPHESIANS 1:4

Before you existed. Before you could do anything good or bad. Before you could choose or refuse. God looked at the emptiness before creation and said your name. And He chose you.

The old God's salvation could be lost — stop choosing Him and His redemption unravels. The new God seals His own. The Holy Spirit Himself is the guarantee, a down payment on a promise that God will finish what He started. His love does not depend on you. His choice of you is irrevocable.

What feels like a smaller God — He does not save everyone? He chooses some? — is a God whose love is particular rather than general. Not a standing offer made to the air. He loved you before the world existed, and He chose you by name.

But What If He Did Not Choose Me?

There it is. That is the sentence underneath all of this, and it is the reason your hands are cold.

Not is this doctrine true — you have half-decided that already, which is why you are still reading. The question is the one you have not said out loud to anybody:

What if He did not choose me?

Because the arithmetic runs both ways, and you did that arithmetic somewhere around Romans 9 and have been carrying it ever since. A doctrine of the chosen implies a doctrine of the not-chosen. And you have no way to read the list.

Anyone who tells you this doctrine is immediately comforting has not stood where you are standing. Do not let them hurry you past it.

Now watch how Jesus handles that exact fear, because He does not do what you would expect. He has just said the most sovereign sentence in the Gospels. And He does not stop there — He adds a second clause, and the second clause is the one nobody quotes:

"All those the Father gives me will come to me, and whoever comes to me I will never drive away."

JOHN 6:37

Read the second half slowly. Whoever comes to me I will never drive away.

Notice what He does not say. He does not say come, and I will check whether your name is on it. He does not say if you are one of mine, you may approach. The list is real — He named it in the same breath — and He never once uses it as a bar at the door. In two thousand years nobody has been turned away from Christ for arriving with the wrong credentials, the wrong feelings, or too late.

So you do not settle this by auditing yourself. You were never handed the decree to read; you were handed a door and a promise about what happens to anyone who walks through it. The way to find out whether you are among the given is not to search your interior for evidence. It is to come — and discover, on the other side, that you were brought.

And if you feel nothing at all reading that — no lift, no relief, only the same flat dread you came in with — that is not a verdict. Nobody asked Lazarus how he felt about the voice, and it made no difference at all to what the voice did. The light on the Damascus road did not wait for Saul to be in a better mood. Coming is not a temperature you have to reach. It is a direction you face.

What to Do While the Ground Is Still Moving

Stay in the text. Not commentaries, not books about books — the passages themselves, the ones frightening you. The Bible that is shaking you and the Bible that is holding you are not two different books, and from inside the shaking you will not be able to tell which it is doing until much later.

And do not rush to resolve the tension. Most people end up with a theology they never examined because they could not bear an open question and took the first answer that closed it. Sit in it. God is not threatened by your wrestling, and an answer that arrives after a year of honest waiting carries weight a borrowed one never will.

Then pray — and pray badly if that is all you have:

"God, if this is true, show me. And if it is true, hold me. I am terrified and confused and alone. But I am willing to follow truth wherever it leads, even if it destroys everything I was taught."

You did not compose that prayer. It was put in your mouth.

"being confident of this, that he who began a good work in you will carry it on to completion until the day of Christ Jesus."

PHILIPPIANS 1:6

The God who opened your eyes to these truths will walk you through every terrifying implication of them. He does not show you truth and then abandon you in it. He finishes what He starts.

You are going to face people who think you have lost your mind, and for a while you are going to be alone in it. But a demolition is what it looks like when an inherited faith is being exchanged for a given one that cannot be taken back — and the exchange is not something you are performing. It is being done to you while you sit there with the book open.

So go back to the verse that started the earthquake, the one that would not say what you were taught. Open your Bible to it again. Put your hand flat on the page. For the first time, probably in your whole life, you are not reading it to defend a position. You are reading it to meet the One who wrote it.

The verse can stay unresolved for another year. Nobody around you has to agree with you yet. Neither of those touches the thing that is actually true, which is that the sovereignty frightening you belongs to a God who did not stay at a safe distance from wreckage. He came down into it in person, took a body, and died inside the ruin His own creatures had made. He has been sitting in the rubble of this house since before the first board gave way.

He has not moved.
