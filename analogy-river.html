The River That Thinks It Chose Its Path

A river flows freely—rushing, turning, cascading over rocks. It appears to go wherever it wants. But every twist, every turn, every rapid was determined by the terrain shaped before the first drop of water fell.

In Brief: A river looks free, and it is genuinely moving — but the terrain settled its course before the first drop arrived, and water has only ever had one appetite: down, away, to the lowest place it can reach. That is dead in sin in a single sentence. So the real question is not whether you chose. It is how a channel ever came to run the other way. It did not erode into place. It was cut through solid rock by Jesus Christ, who went lower than your terrain has ever fallen and came back up — which is why the belief you call yours was water finding a bed God dug in a heart He made new.

Watch a River

A river looks like the freest thing alive. It curves, cascades, braids and splits, crashes against a rock as though the rock had personally offended it. Watch long enough and you begin to believe the river is choosing.

It is not. But that is not the interesting part. The interesting part is that if a river could think, it would be certain that it was.

There is no moment when the current experiences itself as compelled. Every bend feels, from inside the water, exactly like a decision. It simply goes where going is easiest — and easiest is only another word for the shape of the ground.

The Terrain Came First

Uplift and subsidence, hard rock and soft, high ground raised and low ground sunk: the lowest line through the valley was drawn before there was any water to follow it. Only then did the water arrive.

Now a fair objection, and this page will not dodge it. Rivers also carve. The Colorado cut the Grand Canyon, and anyone who has stood on that rim knows the water was not merely a passenger.

True. And it changes nothing that matters. The river deepens its bed, but it never chose the first slope, and it has not once in the history of the world reversed the direction of down. Every canyon it cuts, it cuts lower. All that immense carving power is spent making the original decision more permanent. The water is genuinely working. It is working the terrain's will.

The water's freedom and its total determinism are the same thing. The river is genuinely flowing. Its power is real. Its movement is actual. But it flows only where the terrain allows — and the terrain was there first.

Where This Stops Being About Rivers

Be careful here, because there is a cheap version of this argument and it is not the one Scripture makes. The cheap version says genes and neurons and upbringing settled everything in advance, so nobody was ever free. That is ordinary determinism, and it has no God in it at all — only a longer chain of dominoes with nobody at the end of it who loves you. It can flatten a man's pride. It cannot save him, and it was never meant to.

Scripture says something stranger and immeasurably better. Not that the terrain happened, but that it was carved — deliberately, by a Person, with intent, before there was water to run in it. That is the whole distance between fate and a Father. Fate has a mechanism. This has a face.

So your choices are real. Genuinely, actually yours; you will feel every one of them from the inside as a decision, because it is one. But they run in a bed cut before you got here, by Someone who was not guessing.

What Scripture Actually Says

Proverbs 21:1

"In the Lord's hand the king's heart is a stream of water that he channels toward all who please him."

Not a stream that God influences. Not a stream that God guides gently. A stream in His hand that He turns wherever He wills. The very image is of a river being directed by sovereign hands. The water is flowing. The water is moving. But it is moving exactly where the Hand turns it.

Jeremiah 10:23

"LORD, I know that people's lives are not their own; it is not for them to direct their steps."

Not that man has a hard time directing his steps. Not that man needs help directing his steps. That it is not in man. The capacity to direct one's own steps belongs not to the creature but to the Creator. Your steps are not yours to direct. They flow where the terrain goes.

Psalm 139:16

"Your eyes saw my unformed body; all the days ordained for me were written in your book before one of them came to be."

Not that God predicted your days. That He ordained them — and then wrote them in. Before you existed, every day was already there.

Every River Runs Down

Here is the part of the picture that has been held back until now, and it is the part that matters most.

Water has one appetite. It has never had another. Set it loose on any terrain on earth and it goes to the lowest available place and stops there — and it does this in perfect freedom, never straining against its nature, never once wishing it were climbing. Nobody has to push a river downhill. Down is the only direction it has ever wanted to go.

That is what Scripture means by dead in sin, and it is why the objection but I still choose things misses the point entirely. Of course you choose. You choose constantly, freely, and they are your choices. The question was never whether the water moves. The question is whether it has ever, on its own, run uphill toward God.

Test it somewhere you actually live rather than in theory. In the last free hour you had — genuinely free, nothing owed to anyone — when did the desire to pray arrive unbidden, the way hunger arrives in a healthy body? Not the guilt about not praying. The appetite itself. For most people, honestly answered, the answer is almost never, and the honest answer is not shameful. It is topography. You went where the ground fell away, and the ground falls away from God.

The Comfort in the Current

Here is what the human heart cannot bear to hear: you are not in control. Your life is not ultimately your decision. Your salvation was not your achievement.

And here is what the human heart cannot bear to hear but desperately needs to know: that is the best news imaginable.

A river without banks is a flood. It is destructive. Purposeless. It destroys what it should nourish. It drowns what it should water. A river with banks—a river that flows where it was meant to flow—is a force of life.

If your life had no sovereignty written into it, you would be chaos. You would be a flood of impulses, wounds, and contradictions — freedom with no direction, agency with no love to guide it, a river gone everywhere and therefore nowhere.

But you have been carved into a canyon. The terrain of your life has been shaped by Someone who saw you before you existed and loved you before you could love Him back. Your life flows toward Him not because you have no choice—you have complete choice within the channel—but because the channel itself is carved in the direction of grace.

Every turn, every cascade, every rapid and pool in your life is not happening in spite of God's sovereignty. It is happening because of it. The freedom you experience is real. The choices you make matter. But they matter because they flow through a channel carved by Someone who knew exactly where this water needs to go.

The Illusion of Alternative

Most people imagine that freedom means having had an alternative choice. That genuine choice requires that at any moment, you could have done otherwise. But that is not how freedom works in the real world. That is libertarian fantasy.

But notice what that would actually require. It would require a choice your character did not produce, your loves did not incline, your wanting had no hand in. A choice, in other words, that was not yours — a coin landing in your chest.

The freest act you have ever performed is the one that came most inevitably out of what you actually love. Which relocates the entire question of your salvation to a single place: who set what you love?

If you are resisting this, stay with the resistance a moment rather than arguing it down. What exactly is being defended? Usually not a doctrine. Usually the position of being the one who did it — the hero of the story, the person entitled to say I found my way here.

And that is worth naming gently, because what sits underneath it is almost never pride in the ugly sense. It is fear, and a reasonable one: if I did not choose this love, was it ever really love? The question deserves an answer instead of a rebuke. Here is the answer. No one has ever been loved for reasons they supplied. Every love worth the name arrived before you could earn it, and the ones you had to earn were the counterfeits.

Who Cut the New Channel

Which leaves the one question this whole picture has been running toward. If water goes down, and your terrain ran away from God, and it ran that way freely for as long as you can remember — then how are you still reading this?

Something cut a new channel.

Not erosion. Erosion only ever deepens the direction already there. Ezekiel saw what this actually takes and reported it as surgery rather than geology: "I will remove from you your heart of stone and give you a heart of flesh." Stone does not wear into flesh. It has to be taken out.

And canyons are never cut for free. Something is always spent to move that much rock. In the one channel that runs toward God, what was spent was not time.

Isaiah 53:5

"But he was pierced for our transgressions, he was crushed for our iniquities; the punishment that brought us peace was on him, and by his wounds we are healed."

Jesus Christ is the reason there is a bed in you running a direction water does not go on its own. He went down — lower than your terrain has ever fallen, past every low place you are afraid of, all the way to a grave — and He came up out of it, and the cut He made coming through runs from exactly where you were to exactly where He is.

So the old fear loses its footing. You were never going to climb. Water does not climb. But you were never asked to, and the Psalmist had already gone looking for the bottom and found it occupied: "if I make my bed in the depths, you are there."

You are the river. Every bend is truly yours. And the reason your life bends toward Him at all is that a canyon was opened through solid rock by a Man who was crushed opening it, with your name on the survey before the world had a shape.

So stop testing whether you are flowing hard enough. Down is no longer away from God. Down is the road He took, and He took it first, and He is standing at the end of your water.

He is downstream. He always was.

He Will Never Let You Go

Your life is not out of control. It is not random. You were chosen, shaped, and sustained by Someone whose love is as relentless as a river. Discover what it means to rest in a sovereignty that will never abandon you.
